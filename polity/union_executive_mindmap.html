Introduction to Union Executive

FOUNDATION
📜
Structure & Constitutional Basis
OVERVIEW▶
Union Executive — Composition
  • President of India (Art 52) — Nominal Executive Head
  • Vice President (Art 63) — Rajya Sabha Chair
  • Prime Minister — Real Executive Head
  • Council of Ministers (Art 74) — Aid & Advice
  • Attorney General (Art 76) — Legal Advisor
Parliamentary System Features
  • Nominal Executive: President — Constitutional Head
  • Real Executive: PM + Cabinet — actual power
  • Cabinet Responsibility to Lok Sabha
  • Collective Responsibility of CoM to Lok Sabha
  • Dual executive (like UK Westminster model)
Constitutional Articles Range
  • Art 52–62: President (election, qualification, term)
  • Art 63–71: Vice President
  • Art 72: Pardoning Power
  • Art 73: Executive Power of Union
  • Art 74–75: Council of Ministers, PM
  • Art 76: Attorney General
  • Art 77–78: Government conduct of business
Key Constitutional Principles
  • President acts on aid and advice of CoM (Art 74)
  • President = Constitutional/Nominal head
  • PM = Head of Government (real executive)
  • 44th Amendment 1978: President must act on CoM advice
  • India = Parliamentary democracy (Cabinet type)

President of India — Constitutional Provisions

ART 52–62
👑
Qualifications, Election & Term
ART 52–62▶
Qualifications (Art 58)
  • Indian citizen
  • Completed 35 years of age
  • Qualified for election as member of Lok Sabha
  • Must NOT hold any office of profit under Centre/State
  • Exception: Sitting President/VP, Governor, Union/State Minister not disqualified
Term & Re-election (Art 56–57)
  • Term: 5 years from date of entering office
  • Re-election: Eligible for re-election (no limit)
  • Can resign by writing to Vice President
  • Continues till successor enters office
  • Can be removed by impeachment (Art 61)
Electoral College (Art 54)
  • Elected members of Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha
  • Elected members of State Legislative Assemblies
  • Elected members of Union Territories with Assembly (Delhi, Puducherry, J&K after 2019)
  • NOT: Nominated MPs, MLCs, Rajya Sabha members from states
Election Formula (Art 55)
  • Value of MLA vote = State Population ÷ (1000 × Total elected MLAs)
  • Value of MP vote = Total MLA votes ÷ Total elected MPs
  • Single Transferable Vote system
  • Proportional representation method
  • Secret ballot voting
Vote Value Formula
MLA Vote Value = State Population ÷ (Elected MLAs × 1000) MP Vote Value = Total MLA votes of all states ÷ Total elected MPs
Uniformity of representation: Each state's MLAs should ideally have equal weight per population. MPs' votes are calibrated to balance this.
⚠️ Electoral College Traps
1. NOMINATED members of Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha, State Assemblies do NOT participate in Presidential election.
2. Members of State Legislative COUNCILS (MLCs) do NOT vote — only MLAs (Legislative Assembly).
3. Only Union Territories WITH Legislatures participate (Delhi, Puducherry, J&K) — NOT all UTs.
Impeachment (Art 61)
Impeachment Process
  • On grounds of "violation of Constitution" only
  • Initiated in either House of Parliament
  • Notice signed by 1/4 of total members of that House
  • 14 days notice to President
  • Passed by 2/3 majority of total membership of House
  • Other House investigates — if 2/3 majority → President removed
Key Distinctions
  • No impeachment in India yet (historically)
  • Both Houses VOTE on impeachment
  • Supreme Court judges removed by simple address of both Houses
  • Unlike US: not a political process, more judicial in character
  • Judiciary NOT involved in impeachment

Presidential Powers — Complete Guide

ALL POWERS
⚡
Executive Powers of the President
EXECUTIVE▶
Executive Powers (Art 53)
  • Executive power of Union vested in President
  • Appoints Prime Minister and other ministers
  • Appoints Governors, AG, CAG, CEC, UPSC Chairman
  • Appoints Chief Justice and Judges of SC/HC
  • Appoints National Commissions (minorities, SC/ST etc.)
  • Appoints Ambassadors and diplomatic representatives
  • Appoints Finance Commission, Planning Commission etc.
Legislative Powers
  • Summons, prorogues, dissolves Parliament
  • Addresses joint sitting of Parliament
  • Sends messages to Parliament
  • Nominates 12 members to Rajya Sabha (art, science, literature, social service)
  • Nominates 2 Anglo-Indians to Lok Sabha (removed by 104th Amend)
  • Promulgates Ordinances (Art 123)
  • Gives assent, withholds assent, returns bills (veto)
Financial Powers
  • Budget presented in his/her name to Parliament
  • Money Bills can be introduced in Lok Sabha only with recommendation
  • Constitutes Finance Commission every 5 years
  • Can make advances from Contingency Fund of India
  • No demand for grant without his recommendation
Judicial Powers (Art 72)
  • Pardon: Complete forgiveness
  • Reprieve: Temporary stay of sentence
  • Respite: Lesser sentence (special circumstance)
  • Remission: Reduction in severity of punishment
  • Commutation: Substitute one punishment for another
  • Power over death sentences
  • Offences against Union law or military courts
Diplomatic Powers
  • Represents India in international affairs
  • Treaties/agreements negotiated in his/her name
  • Sends/receives ambassadors
  • All international treaties require Parliamentary ratification
Military Powers
  • Supreme Commander of defence forces
  • Appoints Chief of Army Staff, Navy, Air Force
  • Declaration of war/peace — in name of President
  • Regulated by Parliament through legislation

"Every Leader Feels Deep Joy Mastering"
Executive · Legislative · Financial · Diplomatic · Judicial · Military

Pardoning Powers — Art 72 vs Governor Art 161
MERCY POWER▶

ReprieveTemporary suspension of death sentencePostpone sentence temporarilyPending mercy petition

⚠️ President vs Governor Pardon Power
President (Art 72) pardons: Court martial/military offences AND offences against Union laws AND death sentence cases.
Governor (Art 161): Pardons offences against STATE laws ONLY. Governor CANNOT pardon death sentence — only President can.
Key difference: Governor cannot pardon death sentence even if the crime was under state law!

Veto Powers & Ordinance Making

LEGISLATIVE POWER
🛡️
Three Types of Veto Power
VETO▶
Veto TypeArticleMechanismEffectExample
Absolute VetoArt 111President withholds assent to bill — permanentlyBill is dead — cannot be revivedUsed for Private Member Bills; rare
Suspensive VetoArt 111Returns bill to Parliament for reconsideration. Parliament passes again (simple majority). President MUST give assent.Temporary delay; overridable by ParliamentRegular bills can be returned once
Pocket VetoArt 111President neither gives assent nor returns bill — keeps it pending indefinitely (no time limit in Art 111)Bill in limbo — constitutional questionIndian Postal Amendment Bill 1986 — Zail Singh
⚠️ Key Veto Traps
1. Money Bills — President CANNOT return. Only options: assent or withhold (absolute veto). No suspensive veto for Money Bills!
2. Constitutional Amendment Bills — 24th Amendment mandates President MUST give assent. No veto possible.
3. No time limit for President to exercise pocket veto (Art 111 doesn't specify duration).
🧠 Veto Memory
"Absolute Stops Permanently, Suspensive Stalls Temporarily, Pocket Pends Perpetually"
Absolute=final death; Suspensive=can be overridden; Pocket=indefinite delay

Key Rules
  • Ordinance ceases if Parliament not convened within 6 weeks
  • Or if both Houses pass resolutions disapproving it
  • President acts on CoM advice for ordinance
  • Cannot amend Constitution via ordinance
  • Cannot be promulgated when Parliament is in session (both Houses)
Limitations & Judicial Review
  • Ordinance cannot be promulgated when one House is in session and other is not (unclear)
  • DC Wadhwa case (1987): Re-promulgating ordinances without Parliamentary approval is unconstitutional
  • Ordinance cannot override constitutional provisions
  • Ordinances subject to judicial review (Krishna Kumar Singh case, 2017)
⚠️ Ordinance Critical Points
Governor also has ordinance power (Art 213) — but subject to President's approval in some cases.
"Both Houses" not in session — ordinance can be promulgated even if ONE House is in session (if the other isn't).
6 weeks — from reassembly of Parliament; not 6 weeks from ordinance date.

Emergency Powers — Complete Guide

ARTS 352, 356, 360
🚨
Three Types of Emergency
EMERGENCY▶
EmergencyArticleGroundsApproved byDuration
National EmergencyArt 352War, external aggression, armed rebellion (44th Amend changed 'internal disturbance')Cabinet + Parliament by 2/3 special majority + absolute majorityInitially 1 month; each extension 6 months with Parliament approval
President's Rule (State Emergency)Art 356Constitutional machinery failed in state (Governor's report or President's own satisfaction)Parliament by simple majority6 months; max 3 years (with SC approval after 1 year)
Financial EmergencyArt 360Threat to financial stability/credit of IndiaParliament by simple majorityIndefinite — continues till revoked; Never imposed in India
📌 Critical Emergency Facts
44th Amendment 1978: Changed "internal disturbance" to "armed rebellion" for Art 352. Made proclamation harder.
First National Emergency: 1962 (China War). Second: 1971 (Pakistan War). Third: 1975 (Internal, controversial — Indira Gandhi era).
Art 358: Art 19 automatically suspended during National Emergency (external). Art 359: President can suspend other FRs (not Art 20 & 21).
Financial Emergency: Never proclaimed in India's history!
National Emergency Effects
  • Centre can give directions to states on any matter
  • Parliament can legislate on State List subjects
  • Art 19 automatically suspended (Art 358)
  • Lok Sabha term can be extended by 1 year at a time
  • State executive continues but Centre can direct
President's Rule Effects (Art 356)
  • President assumes functions of State government
  • State legislature dissolved or suspended
  • Parliament makes laws for the State
  • SR Bommai case (1994): Floor test needed before imposing
  • President's Rule subject to judicial review
🧠 Emergency Articles
"352 National War, 356 State Failed, 360 Financial Frail"
352=National Emergency · 356=President's Rule · 360=Financial Emergency

Vice President of India

ART 63–71
🏅
Vice President — Complete Guide
ART 63–71▶
Qualifications & Election
  • Indian citizen; 35 years of age
  • Qualified to be member of Rajya Sabha
  • Should not hold office of profit
  • Elected by both Houses of Parliament (not state legislatures)
  • Single transferable vote, proportional representation
  • All MPs (elected + nominated) vote — unlike Presidential election
Term & Removal
  • Term: 5 years; eligible for re-election
  • Can resign by writing to President
  • Removed by resolution of Rajya Sabha (effective majority + agreed by Lok Sabha)
  • 14 days notice required
  • No specific grounds mentioned — political process
Powers & Functions
  • Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha
  • Acts as President when: office vacant, President away/ill
  • As Acting President: has all presidential powers
  • Does NOT resign from RS chairmanship when acting as President
  • Salary: as RS Chairman, not as VP (when VP only)
⚠️ VP Election Traps
1. VP election: BOTH Houses of Parliament vote — INCLUDING NOMINATED members. This differs from Presidential election (no nominated MPs).
2. State legislators do NOT vote in VP election (unlike President's election).
3. VP resignation: writes to President (not Rajya Sabha). President resignation: writes to VP.

Prime Minister of India

ART 74–75
👤
PM — Appointment, Powers & Role
REAL EXECUTIVE▶
Appointment (Art 75)
  • Appointed by President
  • Must be member of either House of Parliament
  • Must command majority in Lok Sabha
  • If no clear majority: President exercises discretion
  • PM need not be from Lok Sabha — can be from Rajya Sabha (Dr. Manmohan Singh)
Powers & Functions
  • Head of Council of Ministers
  • Chairs Cabinet meetings; coordinates policy
  • Principal communicator between President and Council
  • Allocation of portfolios to ministers
  • Can request President to dissolve Lok Sabha
  • Leader of Lok Sabha; governs Parliamentary proceedings
  • Key role in all appointments made by President
Position in Parliament
  • De facto head of Parliamentary majority
  • Represents India in international forums
  • Leader of the House (Lok Sabha usually)
  • Controls Parliamentary agenda through party
  • No formal upper age limit or educational qualification
PM-President Relationship
  • PM advises President on all appointments
  • President acts on PM/CoM advice (Art 74)
  • 44th Amendment: President CAN refer CoM advice for reconsideration ONCE — but must then accept
  • President acts on Cabinet advice even if advice is not in national interest (constitutionally)
📌 Key Facts about PM
Shamsher Singh case (1974): President must act on CoM advice. PM is real head.
Article 75(3): CoM collectively responsible to Lok Sabha — PM heads this.
Resignation: PM resigns → entire CoM falls. Individual minister resignation → only that minister falls.
PM from Rajya Sabha: Can be PM but answers Question Hour from RS, not LS (limitation).

Council of Ministers & Cabinet

ART 74–75
🗂️
Council of Ministers — Categories & Principles
CoM▶
Three Categories (Art 75)
  • Cabinet Ministers: Heads of important ministries; attend Cabinet meetings; highest decision-making power
  • Ministers of State: Independent charge OR assist Cabinet minister; may/may not attend Cabinet meetings
  • Deputy Ministers: Assist Cabinet/MoS; do not attend Cabinet meetings
  • Parliamentary Secretaries: Not mentioned in Constitution (Art 75)
Key Constitutional Principles
  • Art 74: CoM to aid and advise President — advice is binding
  • Art 75(3): CoM collectively responsible to Lok Sabha
  • Art 75(2): Ministers hold office during pleasure of President
  • 91st Amendment 2003: CoM size = max 15% of Lok Sabha strength (not below 12 for states)
  • Defectors cannot be ministers
Collective Responsibility
  • Cabinet decisions binding on ALL ministers
  • Ministers must defend decisions in public or resign
  • No-confidence motion against entire CoM (not individual)
  • If PM loses majority → entire CoM falls
  • "Cabinet solidarity" principle
Individual Responsibility
  • Each minister responsible for own ministry
  • Can be asked to resign for personal misconduct
  • PM can ask any minister to resign
  • Parliament can censure individual minister
  • Does NOT bring down entire CoM
📌 Cabinet vs Council of Ministers
Council of Ministers: Wide body — Cabinet Ministers + MoS + Deputy Ministers. Constitutionally described in Art 74-75.
Cabinet: Smaller inner body of senior Cabinet Ministers. Not separately mentioned in Constitution — evolved conventionally.
Kitchen Cabinet: Even smaller inner circle of trusted advisors to PM. Informal; no constitutional basis.
⚠️ 91st Amendment 2003 — Size Limit
Maximum size of CoM = 15% of total strength of lower House. For Lok Sabha = 15% of 543 = 81 ministers max. For state legislative assemblies — similarly capped. Minimum 12 for small states. This was to check coalition government excess.

Attorney General of India

ART 76
⚖️
Attorney General — Complete Guide
ART 76▶
Appointment & Qualifications
  • Appointed by President of India
  • Must be qualified to be a Judge of Supreme Court
  • Meaning: High Court judge for 5 years OR advocate for 10 years
  • Holds office during pleasure of President
  • No fixed tenure; no security of tenure like judges
Duties & Rights
  • Give legal advice to Government of India
  • Appear in Supreme Court on behalf of Union
  • Represent Union in cases before SC/HC
  • Discharge functions assigned by President
  • Right of audience in all courts in India
  • Right to participate (not vote) in Parliament proceedings
Limitations
  • Cannot advise or hold briefs against Government of India
  • Cannot defend accused persons in criminal proceedings
  • Cannot accept appointment as a director in companies
  • NOT a whole-time government servant — can do private practice
  • Gets remuneration determined by President
AG vs Solicitor General
  • AG: Constitutional post (Art 76)
  • Solicitor General: Statutory post — NOT in Constitution
  • SG assists AG
  • There are also Additional SGs
  • AG = first law officer of India
⚠️ AG Key Traps
AG is NOT a full-time government servant — can do private legal practice (unlike a minister or civil servant).
AG is NOT a member of Parliament — but has right to participate in debates (without voting rights).
AG qualifications = same as SC Judge. State equivalent = Advocate General (Art 165) — qualifications = HC Judge.

    CDS/UPSC Traps & Common Errors

    AVOID MISTAKES
    ⚠️
    20 Deadly Polity Traps — Union Executive
    CRITICAL▶
    🪤 Electoral College — Nominated MPs
    Presidential election: Nominated MPs do NOT vote. VP election: Nominated MPs DO vote. One of the most tested distinctions!
    🪤 MLCs do NOT vote for President
    Only elected members of State Legislative ASSEMBLIES (MLAs) vote in Presidential election. MLCs (upper house members) do NOT participate.
    🪤 Money Bills — No Suspensive Veto
    President CANNOT return Money Bills for reconsideration. Options: give assent OR withhold assent. No suspensive veto for money bills — cannot be sent back.
    🪤 Constitutional Amendment — No Veto
    24th Amendment mandates President MUST give assent to Constitutional Amendment Bills. No veto possible — not absolute, suspensive, or pocket.
    🪤 Governor Cannot Pardon Death Sentence
    Governor's pardoning power (Art 161) covers only STATE law offences. Governor CANNOT pardon death sentences — that power rests exclusively with President (Art 72).
    🪤 Ordinance — "Both Houses not in session"
    Ordinance can be promulgated when Parliament is not in session. The question of what happens when ONE House is in session is debated. Technically: if EITHER House is in session on that subject, ordinance power may be limited.
    🪤 Ordinance 6 Weeks — From Reassembly
    6 weeks is counted from the date Parliament REASSEMBLES (reconvenes), not from the date of ordinance promulgation. This distinction affects its validity period.
    🪤 44th Amendment and Art 74
    44th Amendment 1978 made it explicit: President CAN refer CoM advice back ONCE for reconsideration — but must then act on revised advice. President cannot indefinitely delay. Before this, advice was absolute.
    🪤 PM from Rajya Sabha is Allowed
    PM need not be from Lok Sabha. Dr. Manmohan Singh was PM while being a Rajya Sabha member (2004–2014). Art 75 doesn't specify which House the PM must be from.
    🪤 Impeachment — Violation of Constitution ONLY
    President can be impeached ONLY for "violation of Constitution" — not for any other reason (unlike US impeachment for high crimes and misdemeanors). Very specific ground.
    🪤 Attorney General — Not Full-Time
    AG is NOT a full-time government servant. Can do private practice. Participates in Parliament (not votes). Solicitor General is NOT in Constitution — AG is (Art 76).
    🪤 Emergency Art 352 — Armed Rebellion (not Internal Disturbance)
    44th Amendment 1978 changed "internal disturbance" to "armed rebellion" for Art 352. More stringent. Cannot declare National Emergency for just any internal disturbance.
    🪤 Financial Emergency — Never Imposed
    Financial Emergency (Art 360) has NEVER been imposed in India's history. All three types of emergency facts are confused — national emergency was imposed 3 times; President's Rule many times.
    🪤 Resignation — President to VP, VP to President
    President resigns → writes to Vice President. VP resigns → writes to President. These are reversed in many exam questions deliberately!
    🪤 91st Amendment — 15% Cap on CoM
    91st Amendment 2003 capped CoM size at 15% of Lok Sabha total strength. Also disqualified defectors from becoming ministers. Often confused with other amendments.
    🪤 Shamsher Singh Case — Not About Emergency
    Shamsher Singh (1974) established that President and Governor must act on CoM/CM advice. Nothing to do with emergency. Often confused with SR Bommai (Art 356) by students.
    🪤 President's Oath — CJI Administers
    President's oath is administered by Chief Justice of India. VP's oath is administered by President. PM's oath is administered by President. All Supreme Court judges' oaths: President.
    🪤 President's Rule Max Duration — 3 Years
    Art 356 (President's Rule): Initially 6 months. Each extension requires Parliamentary approval. Maximum 3 years (SC imposed limit after SR Bommai case). After 1 year: requires SC's concurrence for extension.
    🪤 AG — Qualifications Same as SC Judge
    AG must be qualified to be a Judge of the Supreme Court (not HC). SC Judge qualification: HC judge for 5 years OR advocate for 10 years OR distinguished jurist. Many confuse AG with HC judge qualification.
    🪤 Cabinet Secrecy — Art 75(3)
    Art 75(3) says CoM is collectively responsible to Lok Sabha. Cabinet meetings are SECRET by convention. Cabinet discusses freely knowing debates won't be disclosed. Not from Constitution — conventional principle.

    ANALYTICAL▶
    UPSC Q1 · 10 marks GS-II
    "The President of India is a constitutional figurehead, yet the office is indispensable to the functioning of Indian democracy." Examine.
    Answer Framework
    Intro: Nominal head — acts on CoM advice (Art 74, Shamsher Singh 1974). Yet indispensable because: (1) Constitutional continuity — government stability anchor; (2) Emergency powers — Art 352, 356, 360 (though on advice); (3) Pardoning power Art 72 — last resort mercy; (4) Legislative role — ordinances, prorogation, dissolution; (5) Diplomatic representation; (6) Guardian of Constitution — can delay, refer, advise once. Critical: 44th Amend makes advice binding. Yet President = symbol of national unity. Conclusion: Constitutional monarchy equivalent — power lies in convention and prestige.
    UPSC Q2 · Analytical
    Critically analyze the emergency provisions under the Indian Constitution with special reference to the safeguards introduced after the 44th Amendment.
    Answer Framework
    Pre-44th Amend: Emergency weak safeguards. 1975 Emergency misuse. 44th Amend safeguards: (1) "Armed rebellion" replaces "internal disturbance"; (2) Cabinet must advise in writing; (3) LS can revoke by simple majority resolution; (4) Art 20 & 21 cannot be suspended. SR Bommai: Floor test before President's Rule; judicial review of Art 356. Financial Emergency (Art 360): Never imposed. Remaining concerns: Governor's report discretion; Centre-state tensions. Conclusion: Better but not perfect — constitutional morality depends on political culture.

    Q3 Answer Framework — PM vs President Power
    Constitutionally: President is head of state, PM is head of government. Art 74: President acts on CoM advice → PM holds real power. Shamsher Singh (1974): Confirmed PM's supremacy. PM powers: (1) Cabinet formation — controls appointments; (2) Parliamentary leadership; (3) Party leadership; (4) International representation; (5) All presidential appointments via advice. President: ceremonial yet important for continuity, national unity. PM appears to be more powerful in practice — but President can delay (once), refuse (extreme cases), and set constitutional parameters. Coalition govt: President's role expands when no clear majority. Conclusion: Constitutional theory gives President primacy; practical reality gives PM power.
