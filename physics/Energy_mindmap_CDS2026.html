Introduction to Energy

Definition · SI units · Dimensions · Physical significance

⚡
What is Energy?FOUNDATION
▶

📌 Definition

Energy is the capacity or ability to do work. It is a scalar quantity that cannot be created or destroyed — only converted from one form to another.

  • SI Unit: Joule (J)
  • CGS Unit: erg (1 J = 10⁷ erg)
  • Dimension: [M L² T⁻²]
  • Same dimensions as Work
  • Scalar quantity (no direction)

📐 The Joule

1 Joule = work done when a force of 1 Newton moves an object through 1 metre in the direction of force.

1 J = 1 N·m = 1 kg·m²·s⁻²
SI unit of both energy and work
  • 1 kJ = 10³ J
  • 1 MJ = 10⁶ J
  • 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
  • 1 cal = 4.18 J
  • 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
Scalar QuantityEnergy has magnitude only, no direction
State FunctionEnergy depends on state, not path taken
EquivalenceMass-energy: E = mc² (Einstein)
Dimensions[M L² T⁻²] — same as Work & Torque
1 eVEnergy of 1 electron through 1 V potential
1 kWh3.6 MJ — commercial energy unit

⚠️ CDS/NDA Trap: Torque vs Energy

Torque (N·m) and Energy (J) have the same dimensional formula [ML²T⁻²] but are completely different quantities! Torque is a vector; energy is scalar. This dimensional similarity is a classic trap in multiple-choice questions.

Kinetic Energy

KE = ½mv² · Derivation · Graphs · Numericals · Rotational KE

🏃
Kinetic Energy — Complete AnalysisMOST TESTED
▶
KE = ½mv²
Translational kinetic energy
m = mass (kg) · v = velocity (m/s)
KE in Joules (J)
KE ∝ m (at constant v)
KE ∝ v² (at constant m)
KE = p²/(2m)
In terms of linear momentum p = mv
p = momentum = mv
So KE = (mv)²/2m = m²v²/2m = ½mv²
Useful when momentum is given!

📐 Work-Energy Derivation of KE

STEP 1
F = ma (Newton's 2nd Law)
STEP 2
v² = u² + 2as → a = (v²-u²)/2s
STEP 3
W = Fs = m·(v²-u²)/2s · s
STEP 4
W = ½mv² - ½mu² = ΔKE ✓

KE vs Velocity Graph

KE vs v is parabolic (quadratic)

KE vs Mass Graph

KE vs m is linear at constant v

🔄 Rotational KE

KE_rot = ½Iω²
I = moment of inertia, ω = angular velocity
  • I = Σmr² (discrete)
  • ω in rad/s
  • Fan blade, spinning top, flywheel
  • Rolling: KE = ½mv² + ½Iω²

⚠️ CDS/NDA KE Traps

  • If velocity doubles: KE becomes 4 times (not 2 times — it's v²!)
  • If mass doubles at same v: KE doubles (linear relationship with m)
  • KE = p²/2m → if momentum same but mass doubles: KE halves
  • KE is always positive (it's v², not v)
  • A body at rest has zero KE but may have PE

⚡ Speed Tricks

  • v doubles → KE ×4 | v triples → KE ×9 | v halves → KE ×0.25
  • Given: KE and m → find v: v = √(2KE/m)
  • Given: KE and v → find m: m = 2KE/v²
  • KE in terms of p: KE = p²/2m (memorize for exam!)

Potential Energy

Gravitational PE · Elastic PE · Applications · Energy storage

🏔️
Potential Energy — Two TypesHIGH YIELD
▶
PE_grav = mgh
Gravitational Potential Energy
m = mass (kg) · g = 9.8 m/s² · h = height (m)
Measured from reference level (usually ground)
Can be negative if below reference level
Independent of path taken to reach height h

Real-Life GPE Examples

  • Water in reservoir: PE → KE in turbine
  • Rock at cliff edge: stored GPE
  • Roller coaster at top: max PE, min KE
  • Satellite in orbit: PE + KE = constant
PE_elastic = ½kx²
Elastic/Spring Potential Energy
k = spring constant (N/m)
x = compression/extension from natural length
Hooke's Law: F = kx (restoring force)
Always positive regardless of +/− x

Spring Energy Insights

  • Double compression: PE becomes 4× (∝ x²)
  • k large = stiff spring = more PE per compression
  • PE_spring = area under F-x graph (triangle = ½Fx = ½kx²)
  • Applications: watch spring, car suspension, bow-arrow

HEIGHT vs ENERGY — FALLING BODY

⚠️ PE Traps

  • GPE = mgh: h is measured from the reference level, not from ground always
  • Spring PE ∝ x² (not x) → double extension = 4× PE
  • GPE can be negative (if object is below reference level)
  • Spring PE is ALWAYS positive (x² is always ≥ 0)
  • At height h/2, PE = mgh/2 (but v ≠ v_max/2!)

Mechanical Energy

ME = KE + PE · Conservation · Roller coaster · Pendulum

⚙️
Mechanical Energy — Sum and ConservationCORE CONCEPT
▶
ME = KE + PE = ½mv² + mgh
Total mechanical energy
In absence of non-conservative forces (friction, air resistance):
ME = constant (conservation of mechanical energy)
ΔKE + ΔPE = 0 → KE gained = PE lost

Conservation of Mechanical Energy

  • Valid when only conservative forces act
  • Conservative forces: gravity, spring force, electrostatic
  • Non-conservative forces: friction, air drag (dissipate energy)
  • With friction: ME_final = ME_initial − work done by friction

Law of Conservation of Energy

Statement · Universal applicability · Examples · Proof

🔄
Conservation of Energy — The Fundamental LawUNIVERSAL LAW
▶

📋 Statement of Law

"Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be converted from one form to another. The total energy of an isolated system remains constant."

E_initial = E_final (isolated system)
Mathematical expression of conservation
KE₁ + PE₁ = KE₂ + PE₂ (no friction)
Or: ΔKE = −ΔPE
With friction: KE₁+PE₁ = KE₂+PE₂ + W_friction

🍎 Falling Body

  • At height h: PE=mgh, KE=0
  • At ground: PE=0, KE=mgh
  • At height h/2: PE=mgh/2, KE=mgh/2
  • Speed just before hit: v=√(2gh)

💧 Hydropower

  • Water at height: PE = mgh
  • Falls through dam: PE→KE
  • Turbine: KE→Electrical energy
  • Efficiency η = E_out/E_in × 100%

☀️ Solar Energy

  • Sun: Nuclear→Radiant (light)
  • Solar panel: Light→Electrical
  • Plant: Light→Chemical (photosynthesis)
  • At every step: total E = constant

🧠 Conservation Mnemonic — "ENIC"

Energy Never Is Created

Energy can't be Created or Destroyed — only Converted between forms. Total always constant in isolated system.

Work-Energy Theorem

W = ΔKE · Derivation · Applications · Graphical meaning

💪
Work-Energy Theorem — W = ΔKEEXAM CRITICAL
▶
W_net = ΔKE = ½mv² − ½mu²
Net work done = change in kinetic energy
W_net = sum of work by all forces
v = final velocity, u = initial velocity
If W_net > 0: KE increases (speed up)
If W_net < 0: KE decreases (slow down)
If W_net = 0: KE unchanged (constant speed)

⚡ Applications

  • Stopping distance: W = −½mv² (brakes do negative work)
  • Friction: W_friction = μmg × d (energy dissipated)
  • If friction present: v = √(u² − 2μgd)

📐 Work = Area Under F-d Graph

Area under F-d graph = Work done

⚠️ Work-Energy Traps

  • Work is NOT a vector. W = Fd·cosθ (scalar result, but can be negative)
  • Negative work does NOT mean negative energy — it means energy is removed from the object
  • If velocity is perpendicular to force: W = 0 (circular motion, normal force)
  • Work by gravity: depends only on vertical displacement, NOT path

Heat & Thermal Energy

Q = mcΔT · Specific heat · Heat transfer modes

🌡️
Thermal Energy — Key Concepts & FormulasNUMERICALS
▶
Q = mcΔT
Heat energy absorbed/released
Q = heat (J) · m = mass (kg)
c = specific heat capacity (J/kg·K)
ΔT = temperature change (K or °C)
For water: c = 4200 J/kg·K (very high!)
Q = mL
Latent heat (phase change)
L = latent heat (J/kg)
Latent heat of fusion (ice): 3.36×10⁵ J/kg

🌡️ Heat Transfer Modes

⚠️ Heat vs Temperature Trap

Heat (Q) is energy in transit (Joules). Temperature (T) is measure of hotness (Kelvin/°C). Same Q given to different masses → different ΔT!

Nuclear Energy

E = mc² · Fission · Fusion · Reactors · India's Nuclear Programme

☢️
Nuclear Energy — Einstein's RevolutionSTAR TOPIC
▶
E = mc²
Mass-energy equivalence (Einstein, 1905)
E = energy (J) · m = mass (kg)
c = speed of light = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
c² = 9 × 10¹⁶ m²/s²
1 kg of mass = 9 × 10¹⁶ J of energy!
Nuclear energy comes from MASS DEFECT

Mass Defect & Binding Energy

  • Mass defect: Mass of nucleus < sum of constituent nucleons
  • Δm = mass defect
  • Binding Energy = Δm × c²
  • More binding energy = more stable nucleus
  • Iron-56: most stable nucleus

💥 Nuclear Fission

  • Heavy nucleus splits into smaller fragments
  • Releases huge energy + neutrons
  • Chain reaction: 2-3 neutrons per fission
  • Example: U-235 + n → Ba + Kr + 3n + 200 MeV
  • Used in: nuclear reactors, atom bomb

☀️ Nuclear Fusion

  • Light nuclei combine to form heavier one
  • Requires extremely high temperature (~10⁷ K)
  • Example: 4H → He + energy
  • Sun's energy source!
  • H-bomb (uncontrolled)
  • ITER: controlled fusion research

⚠️ Nuclear Energy Traps

  • Fission = heavy nucleus splitting | Fusion = light nuclei combining (don't confuse!)
  • Sun uses FUSION, not fission
  • Nuclear reactor uses FISSION (controlled chain reaction)
  • Atom bomb: uncontrolled fission | H-bomb: uncontrolled fusion (triggered by fission)
  • India has world's largest Thorium reserves (not Uranium!)

Common CDS/NDA/UPSC Traps

KE-momentum · Work errors · Unit confusion · Power-energy mix-up

⚠️
Top Physics Traps — Don't Lose These Marks!CRITICAL
▶

Trap 1: KE and Momentum

  • Same KE ≠ same momentum: KE = p²/2m → p = √(2m·KE)
  • If KE is same: heavier body has MORE momentum (p ∝ √m)
  • If momentum is same: lighter body has MORE KE (KE ∝ 1/m)
  • Doubling v → KE ×4, but p ×2

Trap 2: Work by Gravity

  • Work by gravity depends only on vertical displacement
  • Path doesn't matter — gravity is a conservative force
  • W_gravity = mgh (h = net vertical drop)
  • On circular path: W_gravity = 0 (no net vertical displacement)

Trap 3: Power vs Energy

  • Power = rate of energy, Energy = power × time
  • A 100W bulb uses 100J per second
  • "More powerful" ≠ "more energy" if time differs
  • kWh is unit of ENERGY, not power!

Trap 4: Dimensions Trap

  • Work, Energy, Torque, Heat all have dimensions [ML²T⁻²]
  • But they are physically different quantities!
  • Power = [ML²T⁻³] | Force = [MLT⁻²]
  • Pressure = Energy density = [ML⁻¹T⁻²]

Trap 5: Nuclear Confusion

  • Fission (heavy → light) used in reactors
  • Fusion (light → heavy) in stars and H-bomb
  • India's Thorium reserves, NOT uranium reserves, are largest
  • Nuclear energy: most energy per unit mass

Trap 6: Renewable ≠ Inexhaustible

  • Biomass is renewable but also polluting
  • Hydropower requires rivers — not available everywhere
  • Tidal energy: gravitational PE of Moon-Earth-Sun system
  • Geothermal: NOT affected by weather (unlike solar/wind)

    40 Probable CDS/NDA Physics Questions

    Click each to reveal answer and explanation

    Q1
    A body of mass 2 kg moving with velocity 4 m/s. What is its kinetic energy?

    Answer: 16 J

    KE = ½mv² = ½ × 2 × 4² = ½ × 2 × 16 = 16 J
    Q2
    If the velocity of a body is doubled, its kinetic energy becomes:

    Answer: 4 times

    KE = ½mv² ∝ v²
    If v → 2v: KE → ½m(2v)² = 4 × ½mv² = 4 × original KE
    Q3
    A ball of mass 0.5 kg is thrown vertically upward with velocity 20 m/s. What is the maximum height reached? (g = 10 m/s²)

    Answer: 20 m

    Using energy conservation: KE = PE at max height
    ½mv² = mgh → h = v²/2g = 400/20 = 20 m
    Or: v² = u² − 2gh → 0 = 400 − 20h → h = 20 m
    Q4
    The SI unit of power is:

    Answer: Watt (W) = Joule/second = J/s

    Power = Work/Time = J/s = Watt
    James Watt, Scottish engineer → unit named after him
    1 HP = 746 W | 1 kW = 1000 W
    Q5
    A motor lifts 200 kg mass through 10 m in 20 s. What is the power of the motor? (g = 10 m/s²)

    Answer: 1000 W = 1 kW

    Work done W = mgh = 200 × 10 × 10 = 20,000 J
    Power P = W/t = 20000/20 = 1000 W = 1 kW
    Q6
    1 kWh = ? Joules

    Answer: 3.6 × 10⁶ J = 3.6 MJ

    1 kWh = 1 kW × 1 hour = 1000 W × 3600 s
    = 1000 × 3600 J = 3,600,000 J = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
    Q7
    Which of the following is NOT a unit of energy? (a) Joule (b) Watt (c) kWh (d) erg

    Answer: (b) Watt — it is a unit of POWER, not energy

    Joule = SI unit of energy
    kWh = commercial unit of energy (= 3.6 MJ)
    erg = CGS unit of energy (1 J = 10⁷ erg)
    Watt = unit of power (= J/s) — NOT energy!
    Q8
    A spring with spring constant k = 200 N/m is compressed by 0.1 m. What is the elastic PE stored?

    Answer: 1 J

    PE = ½kx² = ½ × 200 × (0.1)² = ½ × 200 × 0.01 = 1 J
    Q9
    A 60 W bulb is used for 5 hours daily for 30 days. Calculate the energy consumed in kWh.

    Answer: 9 kWh

    Energy = P × t = 60 W × (5 × 30) hours
    = 60 W × 150 h = 9000 Wh = 9 kWh
    Q10
    What is the kinetic energy of a body when its momentum is p and mass is m?

    Answer: KE = p²/(2m)

    p = mv → v = p/m
    KE = ½mv² = ½m(p/m)² = ½m × p²/m² = p²/(2m)
    This is a very important alternative formula!
    Q11
    At what height does a body have the same kinetic and potential energy? (Started from rest at height H)

    Answer: h = H/2

    At height h: PE = mgh, KE = mg(H-h)
    For KE = PE: mg(H-h) = mgh
    H-h = h → H = 2h → h = H/2
    Q12
    An electric motor of efficiency 80% has output power of 4000 W. What is the input power?

    Answer: 5000 W
