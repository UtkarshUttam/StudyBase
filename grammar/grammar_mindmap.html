Nouns — Complete Master Guide

TYPES & RULES
🔤
Types of Nouns — Full Classification
TYPES▶
Proper Noun

Name of specific person, place, thing. Always capitalized.
Examples: Delhi, Rahul, Taj Mahal, Amazon
⚠️ No article before names: NOT "the Rahul"

Common Noun

General name for any person, place, thing of same kind.
Examples: city, boy, river, table
✓ Can be made plural; takes articles

Collective Noun

Name of a group of people/things.
Examples: team, army, committee, jury, flock
Key: Can be singular OR plural depending on context

Abstract Noun

Name of quality, idea, feeling — cannot be touched.
Examples: honesty, love, courage, freedom
⚠️ Usually uncountable; no plural

Material Noun

Name of material/substance things are made of.
Examples: gold, water, wood, iron, cotton
⚠️ Uncountable; no article 'a/an' before them

Countable vs Uncountable

Countable: apple → apples, book → books
Uncountable: water, air, advice, information, furniture, luggage
⚠️ "Advices" / "informations" = WRONG

⚠️ Most Tested Traps
Data: "The data is correct" (modern) vs "The data are correct" (formal). Both acceptable.
Criteria: One criterion, two criteria. Never "one criteria."
News: "The news IS" (not "are"). News = singular.
Phenomena: "This phenomenon" NOT "this phenomena."
🔗
Possessive Nouns & Compound Nouns
POSSESSION▶
Possessive Rules
Singular noun → add 's: the boy's book, India's capital
The manager's office is large.
Plural noun ending in -s → add ' only: the boys' school, workers' union
The teachers' lounge is on the second floor.
Irregular plural not ending in -s → add 's: children's toys, men's room
Women's rights are fundamental.
⚠️ Apostrophe Trap
its (possessive) vs it's (it is). their (possessive) vs they're (they are) vs there (place). Classic CDS error spotting question!

⚠️ Who vs Whom
Who = subject (nominative): "Who is there?" | "The man who called."
Whom = object (accusative): "Whom did you call?" | "The man whom I met."
Trick: If you can replace with HE/SHE → use WHO. If HIM/HER → use WHOM.
⚠️ Between You and Me (NOT I)
After prepositions (between, for, with, to, from), always use object form. "Between you and me" NOT "between you and I." "It is for him and me" NOT "he and I."
⚠️ Reflexive Pronoun Misuse
WRONG: "Please contact myself for queries." RIGHT: "Please contact me for queries." Reflexive pronouns CANNOT replace personal pronouns when there's no same subject to reflect on.
🔗
Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement — Critical Rules
AGREEMENT▶
Rule 1: Pronoun must agree with antecedent in number, gender, and person.
Every student must submit his/her assignment. (NOT their)
Rule 2: Indefinite pronouns (everyone, each, anyone, nobody) → singular pronoun.
Everyone should do his or her best. (Formally correct)
Rule 3: Collective nouns → singular pronoun (usually).
The team won its match. (NOT their)
Rule 4: Either/Neither → singular pronoun.
Neither of the girls has done her homework.
Rule 5: Compound antecedent joined by 'and' → plural pronoun.
Ram and Shyam have finished their work.

Subject-Verb Agreement — All Rules

SVA MASTER
⚡
Basic & Advanced SVA Rules — Complete Engine
30 RULES▶
BASIC RULES
Rule 1: Singular subject → singular verb; Plural subject → plural verb.
The dog barks. The dogs bark.
Rule 2: "Along with / together with / as well as / in addition to / besides / accompanied by" → verb agrees with FIRST subject.
The teacher, along with his students, has arrived. (NOT have)
Rule 3: Either-Or / Neither-Nor → verb agrees with NEARER subject.
Either he or his friends are responsible. | Neither the boys nor the girl was present.
Rule 4: Indefinite pronouns (everyone, everyone, anyone, nobody, each, someone) → SINGULAR verb.
Everyone has a role to play. Nobody knows the answer.
Rule 5: Collective nouns → singular verb (usually).
The committee has decided. (Acting as one unit)
Rule 6: Collective noun → plural verb when members act individually.
The jury were divided in their opinions. (Each member separately)
Rule 7: Nouns plural in form but singular in meaning → singular verb.
Mathematics is my favourite subject. The news is shocking.
Rule 8: "There" / "Here" → verb agrees with subject AFTER the verb.
There is a book on the table. There are books on the table.
Rule 9: Amount of money, time, distance, weight → treated as SINGULAR.
Five thousand rupees is a large sum. Ten miles is too far to walk.
Rule 10: Two subjects joined by "and" → plural verb.
Ram and Shyam are good friends.
ADVANCED RULES
Rule 11: Two subjects joined by "and" referring to SAME person/thing → singular verb.
The President and Prime Minister of India is different persons… Wait — "The bread and butter is my breakfast."
Rule 12: Fraction / Percentage → depends on the NOUN that follows.
Two-thirds of the students were absent. | Two-thirds of the water was wasted.
Rule 13: "A number of" → plural verb. "The number of" → singular verb.
A number of students have qualified. | The number of students has increased.
Rule 14: "Many a" + singular noun → singular verb.
Many a student has failed. (NOT have)
Rule 15: "More than one" + singular noun → singular verb.
More than one candidate has applied. (NOT have)
Rule 16: Titles of books, movies, countries → singular verb (even if plural-sounding).
"The Arabian Nights" is an interesting book. "The United States" is a superpower.
Rule 17: "One of the + plural noun" → plural noun, singular verb.
One of the students has won. (NOT have — One is subject)
Rule 18: "None" = "not one" → singular verb (traditionally); plural also accepted.
None of the money was found. (Formal)
Rule 19: Relative pronoun (who, which, that) → verb agrees with antecedent.
He is one of those leaders who inspire trust. (leaders inspire)
Rule 20: "Both...and" → plural verb always.
Both the teacher and the student are present.
⚠️ The Golden Rules — Tested in Every CDS Paper
1. "Along with/as well as" → ignore the phrase, verb with FIRST subject
2. "Either/Neither...or/nor" → verb with NEARER (closer) subject
3. "A number of" (plural) vs "The number of" (singular)
4. "Many a" → always singular
5. "More than one" → always singular
6. Money/Distance/Time → singular
7. News/Mathematics/Physics → singular

"A Number Marks Every Individual — Many More Need Singular!"
A number of(plural) · Many a(singular) · More than one(singular) · Neither/Either(nearer) · Indefinite pronouns(singular)

Error Spotting System

DETECTION
🔍
Step-by-Step Error Detection Method
STRATEGY▶
📌 5-Step Error Spotting Method
Step 1: Identify the subject. Strip away phrases like "along with," "as well as," etc.
Step 2: Check subject-verb agreement. Is the verb singular/plural correctly?
Step 3: Check pronoun-antecedent agreement. Does pronoun match?
Step 4: Look for article errors, tense consistency.
Step 5: Check for preposition errors, idiomatic usage.
Common Noun Errors
  • Using plural for uncountable: informations ✗
  • Using article before material noun: a water ✗
  • Wrong plural: criterias ✗ → criteria
  • Phenomenon vs phenomena confusion
Common Pronoun Errors
  • Who vs Whom: Who did you meet? ✗ (Whom)
  • Misusing reflexive: Contact myself ✗
  • Wrong case after preposition: between you and I ✗
  • Pronoun-antecedent mismatch
Common SVA Errors
  • "Along with" → treating as plural subject
  • "A number of" → using singular verb
  • "Many a" → using plural verb
  • Collective noun + plural verb (when acting as unit)

CDS/NDA Grammar Traps

AVOID ERRORS
⚠️
20 Deadly Grammar Traps in Competitive Exams
CRITICAL▶
🪤 "Along with" Trap
The teacher, along with the students, has (NOT have) arrived. The subject is "teacher" — singular. "Along with" phrase is parenthetical, doesn't change verb.
🪤 "A number of" vs "The number of"
A number of students have (plural) passed. | The number of applicants has (singular) increased. Classic CDS trap!
🪤 "Many a" Always Singular
Many a student has (NOT have) failed this exam. "Many a" + singular noun = singular verb always.
🪤 "More than one" — Singular Surprise
More than one candidate has applied. Despite "more than one" suggesting plurality, it takes singular verb. Logical vs grammatical!
🪤 Collective Noun Context
"The team has won its match" (unit). vs "The team were unable to agree" (individuals). Context determines singular/plural.
🪤 "Between You and Me" Not I
After prepositions, use object pronouns: between you and me, not "I." With him and me, not "he." This is tested in error spotting constantly.
🪤 "Who" as Subject Inherits Antecedent's Number
"He is one of those leaders who inspire (not inspires) trust." The "who" refers to "leaders" (plural) — so plural verb.
🪤 "Criteria" is Plural
"The criteria is" = WRONG. Criteria = plural; criterion = singular. "The criteria are clear." Similarly: phenomena (plural), phenomenon (singular).
🪤 Reflexive Pronoun Overuse
"Please send the report to myself" = WRONG. Use "me." Reflexive = only when action reflects back on subject doing it.
🪤 "None" — Singular or Plural?
"None of the books was damaged" (traditional/formal). Modern English allows "were." In exams, treat "none" as singular when "of + uncountable noun" follows.
🪤 Its vs It's
its = possessive (The dog wagged its tail). it's = it is (It's raining). No apostrophe in possessive "its." Their/there/they're similarly confused.
🪤 Either/Neither Subject with Or/Nor
Either he or they are responsible. Neither she nor I am going. Verb agrees with the NEARER subject.
🪤 Money/Distance/Time = Singular
"Five hundred rupees is a large amount." "Ten kilometers is too far." Though plural in form, treated as singular unit.
🪤 "The Police/The Military" → Plural
"The police are investigating." Police, military, staff (collectively referring to individuals) take plural verb in standard usage.
🪤 Titles of Books/Movies → Singular
"The Arabian Nights is a classic." "Three Musketeers is an adventure." Title = one work = singular verb.

Speed Shortcuts — Exam Hall Grammar Hacks

SPEED
⚡
Grammar Speed Techniques
HACKS▶
Subject Finder Method
  • Cross out all prepositional phrases
  • Cross out "along with/as well as" clauses
  • What remains = true subject
  • Match verb to THAT subject
Who/Whom Quick Test
  • Rephrase as statement
  • If answer = HE → use WHO
  • If answer = HIM → use WHOM
  • "He called" → Who called (not Whom)
A/The Number Quick Rule
  • A number of = plural (many)
  • The number of = singular (a count)
  • Memory: "A" = many; "The" = one specific count
Error Spotting Checklist
  • 1. Subject-verb agreement first
  • 2. Pronoun case (who/whom, I/me)
  • 3. Article (a/an/the)
  • 4. Preposition choice
  • 5. Tense consistency
