Constitutional Background

PART IV · ART. 36–51
DIRECTIVE PRINCIPLES OF STATE POLICY — The Conscience of the Constitution
Origin & Borrowing
Primary Source: Irish Constitution (1937)
Ireland borrowed the concept from Spanish Constitution of 1937
Spain → Ireland → India (the chain of inspiration)
Philosophical Sources
Gandhian Philosophy → Village Panchayats, Khadi, Prohibition, Cow protection
Socialist Principles → Welfare state, equitable distribution, labor rights
Liberal-Intellectual tradition → UCC, environment, international peace
Nehru's vision → Planned economy, social justice, secular state
TRAP: DPSP is from Irish Constitution, NOT Spanish. Spanish Constitution influenced Ireland, which then influenced India.
Why Non-Justiciable Yet Included?
Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's Argument
"Novel feature" — a middle path between justiciable rights and mere pious declarations
India was too poor to make all social-economic rights enforceable immediately
Left to the government of the day to implement as resources permit
Granville Austin's View
DPSP + Fundamental Rights = "Conscience of the Constitution"
Together they form the "Core of the commitment to social revolution"
MEMORY
"India was poor but had big dreams — DPSP are those dreams written into law."
Constituent Assembly Debates — Key Points
SUPPORT
  • Jawaharlal Nehru: "Substance of our future Constitution"
  • K.M. Munshi: Not mere pious wishes — binding in spirit
  • Ambedkar: "Instrument of Instructions" to future governments
CRITICISM
  • K.T. Shah: Called them "pious superfluities"
  • T.T. Krishnamachari: "A veritable dustbin of sentiments"
  • Nasiruddin: Vague and unenforceable
FINAL VERDICT
  • Article 37: State SHALL endeavor — moral obligation
  • Not enforceable in courts BUT fundamental to governance
  • Guide legislative + executive action

Conflict Evolution Timeline
1951
Champakam Dorairajan vs State of Madras
FR prevails over DPSP. Led to 1st Constitutional Amendment (Art. 15(4) added). "FR are the Sun, DPSP are the satellite"
1967
Golaknath vs State of Punjab
Parliament CANNOT amend FR. DPSP cannot override FR. FR are sacrosanct.
1971
25th Amendment + Bank Nationalization
Art. 31C added — laws giving effect to Art. 39(b)(c) shielded from Art. 14, 19.
1973
Kesavananda Bharati — WATERSHED
Basic Structure doctrine. Parliament can amend FR but not basic structure. DPSP and FR must be harmonized. Overruled Golaknath.
1976
42nd Amendment — DPSP over FR
Art. 31C expanded — ALL DPSP given precedence over Art. 14, 19, 31. Attempt to override Kesavananda.
1980
Minerva Mills vs Union of India — FINAL WORD
42nd Amend's blanket expansion of Art. 31C struck down. DPSP and FR must coexist — neither can destroy the other. Harmony doctrine finalized.
Harmony Doctrine — Post Minerva Mills
Golden Rule: DPSP and Fundamental Rights are complementary. Neither is superior. Courts must read laws so as to give effect to BOTH.
  • Art. 31C (as valid): Laws for Art. 39(b)(c) not invalidated by Art. 14/19
  • Art. 13: Laws inconsistent with FR = void — DPSP laws must still pass FR test
  • SC uses DPSP as interpretive tools — rights read expansively
  • Art. 21 (Right to Life) now includes many DPSP via expansive reading
  • Unnikrishnan (1993): Right to education (Art. 45 DPSP) became part of Art. 21 — later formalized by 86th Amend
Exam Gold: "The conflict between FR and DPSP was resolved by Minerva Mills — neither is absolute, both must coexist" — this is the standard answer.
05

Landmark Supreme Court Cases

State of Madras vs Champakam Dorairajan
1951 · First FR vs DPSP conflict
Issue: Madras reservation order (caste-based seats in college) — violated Art. 15/29
SC held: FR prevail over DPSP. Communal order invalid. Parliament immediately passed 1st Constitutional Amendment adding Art. 15(4) to enable reservations.
⚡ First amendment was a direct response to SC ruling — DPSP triggered constitutional amendment
Golaknath vs State of Punjab
1967 · Parliament cannot touch FR
Issue: Can Parliament amend Fundamental Rights to implement DPSP?
11-judge bench: No. FR are transcendental — Parliament has NO power to abridge/abrogate them even for DPSP. FR superior to DPSP.
⚡ Led to 24th Amendment (1971) asserting Parliament's amending power. Overruled by Kesavananda 1973.
Kesavananda Bharati vs State of Kerala
1973 · Most important constitutional case
Issue: Extent of Parliament's power to amend Constitution (esp. Kerala land reform laws)
13-judge bench (7:6): Parliament can amend any part of Constitution BUT cannot destroy its "Basic Structure." DPSP and FR must harmonize. Overruled Golaknath.
⚡ Basic Structure doctrine born here. DPSP implementation cannot destroy FR's basic structure.
Minerva Mills Ltd vs Union of India
1980 · Harmony doctrine finalized
Issue: Validity of 42nd Amendment giving DPSP blanket precedence over Art. 14, 19
SC struck down Sec. 4 and 55 of 42nd Amendment. Balance between FR and DPSP is part of Basic Structure. Neither can be used to destroy the other.
⚡ FINAL WORD — harmony between DPSP and FR is a basic feature of Constitution.
NALSA vs Union of India
2014 · DPSP for transgender rights
Issue: Legal recognition and rights of transgender persons
SC used Art. 39A (free legal aid, equal justice) along with Art. 21 to recognize transgender persons as "third gender," directed welfare measures under DPSP framework.
⚡ DPSP used as constitutional basis for social justice to marginalized — modern application
M.C. Mehta vs Union of India (Series)
1986 onwards · Environment DPSP
Issue: Pollution of Ganga, Delhi air quality, Taj Mahal protection
SC invoked Art. 48A (environmental protection) + Art. 51A(g) (citizen duty) + Art. 21 (right to clean environment = right to life) to pass sweeping environmental directives.
⚡ Art. 48A + Art. 21 = right to clean environment is now a Fundamental Right via DPSP.
Unnikrishnan vs State of Andhra Pradesh
1993 · Education as FR
Issue: Right to education — is it a Fundamental Right?
SC: Art. 45 DPSP (free education) read with Art. 21 — right to education up to age 14 is part of Right to Life. Later formalized by 86th Amendment 2002 (Art. 21A).
⚡ Classic example of DPSP elevating into FR via judicial interpretation — Art. 45 became Art. 21A.
Shah Bano Case (Mohd. Ahmed Khan vs Shah Bano)
1985 · UCC controversy
Issue: Maintenance to divorced Muslim woman under Cr.PC Sec. 125
SC awarded maintenance; strongly urged Parliament to enact UCC under Art. 44. Parliament overrode judgment by passing Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act 1986.
⚡ Classic political override of SC on Art. 44 — UCC debate still unresolved. 2023: Law Commission consulted on UCC.

Key Constitutional Amendments Affecting DPSP

42nd Amendment, 1976 — "Mini Constitution"
MOST IMPORTANT
  • Added Art. 39A (free legal aid)
  • Added Art. 43A (workers' participation in management)
  • Added Art. 48A (environment protection)
  • Expanded Art. 31C — ALL DPSP shielded from Art. 14, 19 challenge
  • Added "Socialist" and "Secular" to Preamble
  • Added Fundamental Duties (Art. 51A) — 10 duties originally
⚠️ Art. 31C expansion struck down by Minerva Mills 1980 — only Art. 39(b)(c) retain protection
44th Amendment, 1978
POST-EMERGENCY CORRECTION
  • Added Art. 38(2) — minimize inequalities in status, facilities, opportunities between individuals and groups
  • Deleted Art. 31 (Right to Property) from FR — made it only a legal right under Art. 300A
  • Restored many provisions changed by 42nd Amend
  • Added Art. 20, 21 cannot be suspended even during Emergency
Art. 38(2) is specifically from 44th Amendment — common MCQ!
86th Amendment, 2002
EDUCATION REFORM
  • Inserted Art. 21A — Right to Education (6–14 years) as Fundamental Right
  • Modified Art. 45 — now covers early childhood care for below 6 years only
  • Added Art. 51A(k) — duty of parents to provide education to children 6–14
  • Led to Right to Education Act, 2009
⚠️ After 86th Amend: Art. 45 = below 6 years. Art. 21A = 6–14 years FR. Two different provisions!
97th Amendment, 2011
COOPERATIVES
  • Added Art. 43B — promotion of cooperative societies (latest DPSP addition)
  • Added Part IX-B (Art. 243ZH to 243ZT) for cooperative societies
  • Added "cooperative societies" to Art. 19(1)(c) — right to form cooperatives
Part of SC's Gujarat Cooperative challenge — some state provisions struck down (2021)
Other Significant Amendments
1st AMEND (1951)
  • Added Art. 15(4) for SC/ST reservations after Champakam case
  • Added 9th Schedule (laws beyond judicial review)
25th AMEND (1971)
  • Inserted Art. 31C — laws for Art. 39(b)(c) shielded from Art. 14, 19, 31
  • Reduced "compensation" to "amount" for property acquisition
73rd AMEND (1992)
  • Art. 40 (Panchayats — Gandhian DPSP) given constitutional status
  • Part IX added — made Panchayati Raj justiciable

15 High-Yield Prelims Questions (UPSC/CDS Pattern)

Q.01 · UPSC Pattern
Which of the following Articles was added to the Directive Principles of State Policy by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment, 1976?
1. Article 39A   2. Article 43A   3. Article 48A   4. Article 38(2)
  • 1, 2 and 3 only
  • 1 and 3 only
  • 2 and 4 only
  • 1, 2, 3 and 4
Show Answer & Explanation
ANSWER: A — 1, 2 and 3 only
39A (free legal aid), 43A (workers' participation), 48A (environment) were all added by the 42nd Amendment 1976. Art. 38(2) — minimizing inequalities — was added by the 44th Amendment 1978. This is a classic trap!
Q.02 · CDS Pattern
The Directive Principles of State Policy in the Indian Constitution have been borrowed from the Constitution of:
  • USA
  • Canada
  • Ireland
  • Australia
Show Answer
ANSWER: C — Ireland
DPSP borrowed from Irish Constitution (1937). Ireland was itself influenced by the Spanish Constitution. The chain: Spain → Ireland → India.
Q.03 · UPSC 2022 Pattern
Consider the following statements about Article 45 of the Indian Constitution after the 86th Constitutional Amendment (2002):
1. It now provides for free and compulsory education for children of age 6 to 14 years.
2. It now applies to early childhood care and education for children below 6 years.
3. Education for 6–14 year age group was moved to Article 21A as a Fundamental Right.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  • 1 and 2 only
  • 2 and 3 only
  • 3 only
  • 1, 2 and 3
Show Answer
ANSWER: B — 2 and 3 only
Statement 1 is WRONG — after 86th Amendment, Art. 45 covers only below 6 years. 6–14 years education moved to Art. 21A as Fundamental Right. Statements 2 and 3 are correct.
Q.04 · Classic MCQ
In which landmark case did the Supreme Court establish the "Harmony Doctrine" — that Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles must coexist and neither can destroy the other?
  • Golaknath vs State of Punjab (1967)
  • Kesavananda Bharati vs State of Kerala (1973)
  • Minerva Mills vs Union of India (1980)
  • Champakam Dorairajan vs State of Madras (1951)
Show Answer
ANSWER: C — Minerva Mills vs Union of India (1980)
Minerva Mills (1980) finalized the harmony doctrine — both FR and DPSP must coexist; the balance between them is part of the Basic Structure of the Constitution. The 42nd Amendment's blanket precedence to DPSP over FR was struck down.
Q.05 · CDS Pattern
Which Article of the Indian Constitution directs the State to promote cottage industries on an individual or cooperative basis in rural areas?
  • Article 40
  • Article 43
  • Article 43B
  • Article 46
Show Answer
ANSWER: B — Article 43
Art. 43 covers: (i) living wage for workers and (ii) promotion of cottage industries on individual or cooperative basis in rural areas. Art. 43B (added by 97th Amend 2011) is specifically about promotion of cooperative societies.
Q.06 · UPSC Prelims Pattern
Which of the following correctly describes the provision of Article 39(d) of the Indian Constitution?
  • Adequate means of livelihood for all citizens
  • Equal pay for equal work for both men and women
  • Distribution of material resources to serve the common good
  • No concentration of wealth to the common detriment
Show Answer
ANSWER: B — Equal pay for equal work for both men and women
Art. 39 sub-clauses: (a)=livelihood, (b)=distribution of material resources, (c)=no concentration of wealth, (d)=EQUAL PAY for equal work, (e)=no economic abuse of workers, (f)=children's healthy development. Art. 39(d) is the basis for Equal Remuneration Act 1976.
Q.07 · CDS Trap Question
Article 50 of the Indian Constitution directs separation of judiciary from the executive. This provision applies to:
  • All courts including High Courts and Supreme Court
  • Only the Supreme Court of India
  • Public services of the State
  • Both High Courts and District Courts
Show Answer
ANSWER: C — Public services of the State
Art. 50 applies to PUBLIC SERVICES of the State only — not to High Courts or the Supreme Court, which have their own separate constitutional provisions for independence. This is a favourite exam trap.
Q.08 · Amendment-based
Article 43B relating to promotion of cooperative societies was inserted into the Constitution by the:
  • 73rd Amendment, 1992
  • 86th Amendment, 2002
  • 97th Amendment, 2011
  • 42nd Amendment, 1976
Show Answer
ANSWER: C — 97th Amendment, 2011
Art. 43B is the latest addition to DPSP, inserted by 97th Constitutional Amendment 2011. It also added Part IX-B to the Constitution dealing with cooperative societies.
Q.09 · Case Law Based
The case of Champakam Dorairajan (1951) resulted in which Constitutional Amendment?
  • 7th Amendment adding State Reorganisation provisions
  • 1st Amendment adding Article 15(4) for backward class reservations
  • 24th Amendment reaffirming Parliament's power to amend Constitution
  • 44th Amendment deleting Article 31
Show Answer
ANSWER: B — 1st Amendment adding Article 15(4)
After SC ruled in Champakam that caste-based reservations violated Art. 15/29 (FR prevail over DPSP), Parliament immediately passed the 1st Constitutional Amendment (1951) adding Art. 15(4) — enabling special provisions for socially/educationally backward classes.
Q.10 · UPSC Pattern
The concept that Article 37 makes Directive Principles "fundamental in the governance of the country" while declaring them non-justiciable means:
  • Courts can enforce them directly like Fundamental Rights
  • They create a moral obligation on the State but cannot be enforced in courts
  • Citizens can sue the State for non-implementation
  • They automatically become Fundamental Rights after 10 years
Show Answer
ANSWER: B
DPSP are "fundamental in governance" — they guide legislative/executive action and courts use them as interpretive tools — but they cannot be directly enforced in courts (non-justiciable). However, if legislation is enacted to implement DPSP, that legislation CAN be challenged in courts.
Q.11 · Scheme Linkage
MGNREGA (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act) draws its constitutional backing primarily from which Article?
  • Article 39
  • Article 40
  • Article 41
  • Article 43
Show Answer
ANSWER: C — Article 41
Art. 41 directs the State to make effective provision for securing the right to work "within the limits of its economic capacity." MGNREGA (2005) is the direct legislative implementation of Art. 41 providing 100 days of guaranteed employment.

Q.13 · Shah Bano / Art. 44
After the Supreme Court's judgment in Shah Bano case (1985), Parliament passed the Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act 1986. This was seen by critics as:

MAINS Q.01 · GS-II / 150 words
"The Directive Principles of State Policy are not mere pious declarations but have gradually become enforceable through legislative action and judicial interpretation." Critically examine with examples.
INTRO: Define DPSP — Art. 37 makes them non-justiciable yet fundamental to governance. Quote Granville Austin's "conscience of the Constitution."

BODY — 3 Dimensions:
1. Legislative Transformation: MGNREGA (Art. 41) → right to work legislated; RTE Act (Art. 45 → Art. 21A via 86th Amend); NALSA (Art. 39A); Maternity Benefit Act (Art. 42)
2. Judicial Elevation: Unnikrishnan (Art. 45 → Art. 21); MC Mehta (Art. 48A + Art. 21 = right to clean environment); NALSA (Art. 39A + Art. 21 = transgender rights)
3. Constitutional Interlinkage: Harmony doctrine (Minerva Mills) — DPSP and FR complement each other; Art. 31C protects Art. 39(b)(c) laws

CRITICISM: UCC (Art. 44) unimplemented; living wage (Art. 43) aspirational; uniform civil code debate

CONCLUSION: DPSP represent India's commitment to a welfare state — while the journey from aspiration to implementation is incomplete, the trajectory is clear.
MAINS Q.02 · GS-II / 250 words
Trace the evolution of the relationship between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles of State Policy from Champakam Dorairajan (1951) to Minerva Mills (1980). What is the current constitutional position?
INTRO: Constitutional framers envisioned FR and DPSP as complementary — but early SC judgments created tension. The story is of pendulum swings leading to balance.

EVOLUTION TABLE FORMAT:
1951 Champakam → FR prevails → 1st Amend (Art. 15(4))
1967 Golaknath → FR transcendental, Parliament cannot amend → 24th Amend reaffirms Parliament's power
1971 25th Amend → Art. 31C: DPSP laws shielded from FR challenge
1973 Kesavananda → Basic Structure; FR + DPSP must harmonize; overrules Golaknath
1976 42nd Amend → ALL DPSP given precedence — attempt at supremacy
1980 Minerva Mills → Balance = Basic Structure; 42nd Amend's expansion struck down

CURRENT POSITION:
• Harmony doctrine: neither can destroy the other
• Courts use DPSP to interpret FR expansively (Art. 21 = right to health, education, environment)
• Art. 31C (limited): only Art. 39(b)(c) laws shielded from Art. 14/19
• DPSP guide policy; FR guarantee enforcement

ANALYTICAL POINT: India's constitutional design is unique — it bridges civil-political rights (FR) with socio-economic rights (DPSP) rather than choosing one model.
MAINS Q.03 · GS-I / 150 words
How far have Gandhian Directive Principles been implemented in post-independence India? Are they still relevant in the 21st century?
GANDHIAN DPSP OVERVIEW: Art. 40, 43, 43B, 46, 47, 48 — rooted in gram swaraj, khadi, cow protection, prohibition.

IMPLEMENTATIONS:
• Art. 40 → 73rd Amendment (1992): Panchayati Raj — most successful; 2.5 lakh gram panchayats
• Art. 43 → KVIC, PM Vishwakarma (2023): Khadi still alive; cottage industry support
• Art. 43B → New Ministry of Cooperation (2021); cooperative credit, dairy cooperatives (Amul)
• Art. 46 → SC/ST reservations, Atrocities Act, Scholarships
• Art. 47 → Prohibition in Bihar, Gujarat (partial); POSHAN Abhiyaan
• Art. 48 → State cow slaughter laws; organic farming push

LIMITATIONS: Cow protection politicized; prohibition creates black markets; full village economic self-sufficiency not achieved

21ST CENTURY RELEVANCE: SDG 1 (no poverty) ↔ Art. 43; SDG 16 (institutions) ↔ Art. 40; sustainable agriculture ↔ Art. 48. Gandhian principles remain relevant — just need modern interpretation.
MAINS Q.04 · GS-II / 250 words
"The debate on Uniform Civil Code remains the most contested dimension of the Directive Principles of State Policy." Analyze the constitutional, social, and political dimensions of implementing Article 44.
INTRO: Art. 44 — State shall endeavor to secure a UCC throughout India. In 75+ years, not enacted. Why?

CONSTITUTIONAL DIMENSION:
• Art. 44 vs Art. 25-28 (freedom of religion) — tension at the core
• SC in Shah Bano (1985), Sarla Mudgal (1995), John Vallamattom (2003) — all urged UCC
• Goa has a UCC since colonial times (Portuguese Civil Code)
• Parliament can legislate UCC under Entry 5, List III (Concurrent List)

ARGUMENTS FOR:
• Gender justice — Muslim women, Hindu daughters (equal inheritance)
• National integration — one law for all citizens
• Constitutional mandate (Art. 44), SC directions
• Anomalies: Hindu Code Bills (1950s) reformed Hindu law but personal laws of minorities untouched

ARGUMENTS AGAINST:
• Minority fears: homogenization as cultural erasure
• What will be the "uniform" code — whose customs prevail?
• Federal concern: personal laws partly state subject
• Better approach: optional civil code

RECENT DEVELOPMENTS: Uttarakhand UCC Act (2024) — first state to pass UCC; Law Commission consultation (2023)

CONCLUSION: UCC requires national consensus, not legislative imposition — the journey must be through dialogue, not division.
MAINS Q.05 · GS-III / 150 words
Examine how India's approach to welfare state governance is reflected in the Directive Principles of State Policy. Connect the DPSP framework with India's commitments to the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).
WELFARE STATE CONCEPT: DPSP collectively embody the welfare state ideal — state actively ensures social-economic justice, not just negative freedoms.

DPSP ↔ SDG MAPPING:
SDG 1 (No Poverty) ↔ Art. 39, 41, 43 — livelihood, employment, wages
SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) ↔ Art. 47, PM POSHAN — nutrition and food
SDG 3 (Good Health) ↔ Art. 47, Ayushman Bharat
SDG 4 (Quality Education) ↔ Art. 45, 21A, RTE Act
SDG 5 (Gender Equality) ↔ Art. 39(d), 42 — equal pay, maternity
SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities) ↔ Art. 38, 46
SDG 13 (Climate Action) ↔ Art. 48A
SDG 16 (Strong Institutions) ↔ Art. 40, 50, 39A
SDG 17 (Global Partnerships) ↔ Art. 51

CRITICAL ANALYSIS: India's welfare state remains aspirational — inequality has risen even as DPSP-backed schemes multiply. Need convergence between DPSP intent and governance delivery.

CONCLUSION: DPSP are India's SDG commitment written 75 years before the SDGs — a prescient constitutional vision awaiting full realization.
