Historical Background & Political Awakening

FOUNDATION
📜
Political Atmosphere Before 1885
CONTEXT▶
Socio-Religious Reform Impact

Reform movements created educated middle class: Brahmo Samaj (Bengal), Prarthana Samaj (Bombay), Arya Samaj (Punjab). National consciousness emerged through reformist discourse.

Western Education Effect

English education created class aware of: Liberty, Equality, Rights, Constitutionalism. Led to awareness of contrast between British ideals and Indian reality. Macaulay's education policy → backfired on British.

Economic Discontent

Deindustrialization, drain of wealth, high land taxes, recurring famines created mass discontent. Rising unemployment among educated Indians → fuel for nationalism.

1857 Aftermath

1857 Revolt showed need for organised, systematic national movement. Government became more repressive. Indian intelligentsia sought constitutional, non-violent methods.

⚠️
Ilbert Bill Controversy (1883) — Immediate Trigger
CATALYST▶
📌 Ilbert Bill Context
Ilbert Bill (1883): Drafted by C.P. Ilbert (Legal Member of Viceroy's council under Ripon). Proposed that Indian judges could try European British subjects in criminal cases in mofussil courts.

European reaction: Massive organized opposition. Europeans held meetings, petitioned, organized "White Mutiny." Press campaign against the bill.

Compromise: Bill amended — jury of at least half European membership when Indian judge tries European. Showed organized British opposition could influence policy.

Indian lesson: Indians saw organized agitation worked. Led directly to formation of INC to represent Indian interests systematically.
⚠️ CDS Trap: Ilbert Bill
The Ilbert Bill was proposed under Viceroy Lord Ripon (1880–84), NOT Dufferin. A.O. Hume's INC idea developed during Dufferin's time (1884–88). Ripon was the reform-minded viceroy; Dufferin succeeded him.

Formation of INC (1885)

FOUNDING EVENT
⭐
A.O. Hume & INC Formation
FOUNDING▶
A.O. Hume (1829–1912)

Allan Octavian Hume — Retired British ICS officer. Called "Father of INC." Ornithologist. Met Viceroy Dufferin for guidance. Organized first session in Bombay (1885).
Wrote circular to Calcutta graduates (1883) urging formation of national body.

First Session (Dec 1885)

Date: 28–30 December 1885.
Venue: Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay.
Delegates: 72 (originally planned for Pune, shifted due to cholera).
President: W.C. Bonnerjee (Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee).

Objectives of INC
  • Consolidate national sentiment
  • Frame and present people's demands to government
  • Train Indians in political work
  • Promote friendly relations among nationalist workers
Safety Valve Theory

Lala Lajpat Rai first called INC a "safety valve." Theory: British created INC to provide peaceful outlet for political discontent, preventing revolution.
Evidence: Hume consulted Dufferin; initial British support.

📌 First Session — Key Facts
Original plan: Pune (but cholera epidemic shifted it to Bombay). Delegates: 72 from all over India. No woman delegate in first session. W.C. Bonnerjee was a Calcutta barrister. Session also called "National Conference" initially. Dadabhai Naoroji proposed the name "Indian National Congress."
⚠️ Safety Valve Theory — UPSC Trap
Who gave Safety Valve Theory? Lala Lajpat Rai (in Young India, 1916). NOT Hume himself. Bipin Chandra Pal also supported this critique. The nationalist counter-argument: even if started as safety valve, it became the vehicle of India's freedom movement. This is a Mains question theme.

Moderate Phase (1885–1905)

EARLY INC
🕊️
Moderate Ideology & Approach
IDEOLOGY▶
Core Beliefs
  • Faith in British justice and constitutional methods
  • British rule ultimately beneficial for India
  • Gradual reforms through peaceful agitation
  • Petitions, prayers, memorials to Parliament
  • Use of press & public opinion
Political Methods
  • Petitions to Parliament and Councils
  • Delegations to British officials
  • Press campaigns to educate public
  • Annual Congress sessions for deliberation
  • Deputations to England (Dadabhai elected MP)
Class Base

Largely educated middle class: lawyers, doctors, teachers, journalists. Limited mass contact. Constitutional liberalism strongly influenced by British political thought. Called "3P" policy: Petition, Prayer, Protest.

Assessment

Bal Gangadhar Tilak criticized: "mendicancy." British were unresponsive. But moderates created political consciousness, established INC as institution, and articulated India's economic exploitation.

Demands & Methods of Moderates

POLITICAL PROGRAMME
📋
Key Demands of the Moderate Congress
DEMANDS▶
Constitutional Reforms
  • Expansion of Legislative Councils
  • Elective principle in councils
  • More Indians in Imperial Council
  • Repeal of repressive laws
Administrative Reforms
  • Indianization of Civil Services (ICS)
  • Simultaneous Civil Services exam in India and England
  • Separation of judiciary from executive
  • Freedom of speech and press
Economic Demands
  • Reduce military expenditure (drain on India)
  • Reduce Home Charges
  • Cotton excise duty removal
  • Protection of Indian industries
Other Demands
  • Trial by jury for political cases
  • Arms Act abolition
  • Repeal of Vernacular Press Act
  • India Office cost to be borne by Britain
📌 Indian Councils Act 1892 — Moderate Achievement
Passed as partial response to INC demands. Expanded legislative councils. Allowed discussion of budget. Introduced indirect elections (not direct). Indians could nominate some members. Limitations: No real control over finances, no direct election, no control over executive. Moderates saw it as partial victory; critics called it inadequate.

Economic Critique of British Rule

DRAIN THEORY
💰
Drain of Wealth Theory — Dadabhai Naoroji
CORE THEORY▶
Core Argument
"India's poverty is due to systematic draining of its wealth to Britain"
Dadabhai Naoroji, "Poverty and Un-British Rule in India" (1901). R.C. Dutt "Economic History of India" (1902) supported this.
Channels of Drain
  • Home Charges: Salaries of British officials, pensions paid in England
  • Guaranteed Railway Interest: Profits from railways built with Indian capital
  • Trade surplus: India exported more than imported, but surplus didn't return as capital
  • Payment for India's wars in Asia + Africa
  • India Office cost borne by India
Deindustrialization
  • British machine goods destroyed Indian handicrafts
  • Textile industry of Dacca, Surat, Murshidabad collapsed
  • Free trade policy favored Britain (not India)
  • Cotton excise duties — India taxed own cotton goods
  • Agriculture overcrowding → poverty cycle
Famines (Economic Impact)
  • 25+ famines 1765–1858; 12+ after 1857
  • 1876–78: Madras/Bombay famine (5.5M deaths)
  • 1899–1900: Famine under Curzon
  • Famine Commissions Reports — often ignored
  • Created anti-British sentiment + nationalist fuel
Economic Nationalists
  • Dadabhai Naoroji: Drain Theory, "Poverty and..."
  • R.C. Dutt: "Economic History of India" (2 vols)
  • G. Subramania Iyer: "Our Public Finance"
  • M.G. Ranade: "Essays on Indian Economics"

British Attitude Towards INC

REACTION
⚔️
Evolution of British Response
HOSTILITY GROWS▶
1885–87
Initial Tolerance / Ambiguity
Viceroy Dufferin initially tolerant; saw INC as safety valve. Government did not actively oppose first sessions.
1887–88
First Signs of Hostility
Dufferin called INC leaders "microscopic minority." Encouraged Muslim separatism (Sayyid Ahmad Khan). Discouraged civil servants from attending INC.
1892
Tokenism — Indian Councils Act
Partial reforms given to show British responsiveness. ICA gave limited powers to councils but no real change of power.
1899–1905
Curzon's Repression
Lord Curzon: most repressive policies. Calcutta Corporation Act (1899), Universities Act (1904), Partition of Bengal (1905). Open contempt for INC.
1905
Partition of Bengal — Deliberate Challenge
Curzon used partition to divide nationalist movement along religious lines. Weakened Bengal — the heart of nationalism.
Divide & Rule

British encouraged communal polarization: Sayyid Ahmad Khan's Muslim League politics actively supported. Separate electorates seeded (before 1905). Discouraged Hindu-Muslim joint platform.

Criticism of Congress

Lord Dufferin: "microscopic minority." Curzon: "seditious organization." British Committee (London) actively lobbied against INC demands in Parliament. Vernacular press censored.

Partition of Bengal (1905)

TURNING POINT
🗺️
Bengal Partition — Complete Analysis
1905 EVENT▶
Official Justification

Bengal (1905) had 78 million people — too large to administer efficiently. New partition: Bengal (West) + Assam (East). Curzon claimed administrative convenience.

Real Motive
  • Break the nationalist movement in Bengal (strongest)
  • Divide Hindus (West Bengal) and Muslims (East Bengal)
  • Communal angle: Muslim-majority East Bengal appeased
  • Reduce Bengali babus' political influence
Nationalist Response
  • 16 October 1905 = "Mourning Day" / "National Day"
  • Rakhi Bandhan between Hindus & Muslims — Tagore's idea
  • Mass hartals, public meetings throughout India
  • Swadeshi & Boycott movement launched
Outcome

Partition annulled in 1911 (Delhi Durbar). British capital shifted to Delhi from Calcutta. But Bengal was divided again in 1947 (India-Pakistan) and 1971 (Bangladesh). Shows colonial divide remains.

📌 Bengal Partition — Key Dates
July 19, 1905: Partition announced by Curzon. October 16, 1905: Partition officially implemented = Day of Mourning. Tagore's response: "Amar Sonar Bangla" written as protest song (later became Bangladesh's national anthem). Annulled: December 12, 1911 at Delhi Durbar. British capital also shifted Delhi → Calcutta → Delhi.
⚠️ Bengal Partition Trap
Bengal was partitioned in 1905 (Curzon), annulled 1911. Don't confuse with 1947 partition (India-Pakistan) or 1971 (Bangladesh liberation). Also: Tagore's "Amar Sonar Bangla" written during 1905 anti-partition movement — now Bangladesh's national anthem!

Swadeshi & Boycott Movement

ECONOMIC NATIONALISM
🌿
Swadeshi Movement — Complete Analysis
MOVEMENT▶
Swadeshi (Our Own Country)
  • Use Indian-made goods; boycott British goods
  • Promoted indigenous industries: cotton, matches, salt
  • National schools established
  • Bengal National College founded (1906) — Aurobindo principal
Boycott Programme
  • Burn British cloth publicly
  • Refuse British manufactured goods
  • Boycott of British courts and councils (extremist version)
  • Public bonfires of foreign cloth (precursor to Gandhi's 1920 programme)
National Education
  • Boycott government schools/colleges
  • Bengal National College (1906)
  • Aurobindo Ghosh as principal
  • Precursor to Gandhi's Non-Cooperation education policy
Passive Resistance (Aurobindo)
  • Non-cooperation with British administration
  • Refuse titles, awards, government posts
  • Civil disobedience elements
  • Influenced Gandhi's later Non-Cooperation Movement
🧠 Swadeshi 4 Pillars
"Boycott Swadeshi National Passive"
B=Boycott of foreign goods · S=Swadeshi (use Indian goods) · N=National Education · P=Passive Resistance

CDS/UPSC Traps & Common Errors

AVOID MISTAKES
⚠️
15 Deadly History Traps — Early INC
CRITICAL▶
🪤 INC First Session Location Trap
First session was planned for Pune but shifted to Bombay due to cholera. Questions may say "first session was in Pune" — that's WRONG. It was Bombay (Gokuldas Tejpal College).
🪤 First President Trap
W.C. Bonnerjee was first President (1885). NOT Dadabhai Naoroji (he was 2nd session, 1886). NOT A.O. Hume (he was founder, not president of first session).
🪤 Safety Valve Theory Author
Safety Valve Theory coined by Lala Lajpat Rai (in Young India). NOT by British historians. Some questions attribute it to Bipin Chandra Pal or historians — Lajpat Rai was primary advocate.
🪤 First Muslim President of INC
Badruddin Tyabji (1887) was first Muslim Congress President. NOT Muhammad Ali Jinnah. Jinnah presided much later (1916, Lucknow). Tyabji at Madras session.
🪤 George Yule — First/Only British President
George Yule (1888, Allahabad) was the first and ONLY British/European President of INC. Often confused — people think A.O. Hume was a Congress President. Hume was organizer/founder, not president of any session.
🪤 Amrita Bazar Patrika Language
Amrita Bazar Patrika was ORIGINALLY Bengali. Switched to English overnight to escape Vernacular Press Act (1878). Questions ask "which newspaper became English to escape VPA" — answer is ABP, not Bengalee.
🪤 Dadabhai Naoroji's Parliament Seat
Naoroji was elected MP from Finsbury (Central), London in 1892. He was a Liberal Party MP. First Indian to be elected to British Parliament. Not from any Indian constituency obviously.
🪤 Tilak's "Swaraj" Slogan
"Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it" = Bal Gangadhar Tilak. Dadabhai Naoroji first USED the word Swaraj in INC (1906 Calcutta session). These are different — Tilak's slogan ≠ Naoroji first using the word.
🪤 Bengal Partition Annulment
Bengal Partition (1905) was annulled in 1911 at Delhi Durbar. Don't say it was annulled because of movement alone — the British also shifted capital to Delhi simultaneously, balancing the decision politically.
🪤 Amar Sonar Bangla
Rabindranath Tagore wrote "Amar Sonar Bangla" during the anti-partition movement of 1905. It later became the national anthem of Bangladesh. Jana Gana Mana (Tagore) = India's anthem. Don't mix these up!
🪤 Indian Councils Act 1892 vs 1909
ICA 1892 (Lansdowne) = partial response to INC; indirect elections. ICA 1909 (Minto-Morley) = separate electorates for Muslims. These are completely different acts with different viceroys and implications. Very common confusion!
🪤 Poona Sarvajanik Sabha Founder
Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1867) founded by M.G. Ranade & G.V. Joshi. NOT Tilak. Tilak was associated later. Often attributed to Tilak due to Pune connection — incorrect.
🪤 Gokhale's Organization
Gokhale founded Servants of India Society (1905), NOT Poona Sarvajanik Sabha. His mentor Ranade founded PSS. Common confusion due to both being Pune-based.
🪤 Tilak's Sedition Trials
Tilak was tried for sedition in 1897 (after Chaphekar brothers killed plague officer Rand) and 1908 (for Kesari articles after Muzaffarpur bomb case). 1908 trial → 6 years Mandalay jail. Don't say he was tried once.
🪤 East India Association
East India Association (1866) was founded by Dadabhai Naoroji in London — NOT in India. It lobbied the British Parliament and public, not Indian audiences directly.

UPSC Q1 · 10 marks
"The formation of the Indian National Congress in 1885 was the result of both colonial circumstances and genuine nationalist aspirations." Critically examine.
Answer Framework
Intro: Dual causation → colonial + nationalist. Colonial: Ilbert Bill, educated class, safety valve thesis. Nationalist: Pre-Congress associations, economic grievances, reform movements. Key: Even if British permitted/encouraged, it was filled with genuine nationalist content. Bipin Chandra's interpretation vs Cambridge School. Conclusion: Genesis doesn't determine character — INC's transformation into mass movement proves nationalist authenticity.
Key terms: Safety Valve, ICS discontent, A.O. Hume, nationalist historiography
UPSC Q2 · Analytical
Evaluate the economic critique of British rule developed by early nationalist leaders. How did the "Drain Theory" shape Indian nationalist consciousness?
Answer Framework
Intro: Economic nationalism as intellectual foundation. Naoroji's Drain Theory: channels (Home Charges, railways, trade surplus). R.C. Dutt, M.G. Ranade contributions. Impact: gave scientific basis to anti-British sentiment; united different social classes; made "economic exploitation" central nationalist argument. Swadeshi as practical response to drain. Limitations of theory (some scholars debate quantum). Legacy: influences modern debate on colonial exploitation.
Economic History of India, "Poverty and Un-British Rule", Drain of Wealth channels
UPSC Q3 · Compare
Compare and contrast the Moderate and Extremist phases of the Indian National Congress with reference to ideology, methods, social base, and historical contribution.
Answer Framework
Use comparison table structure: Ideology (partial self-govt vs complete Swaraj) · Methods (3P vs boycott/swadeshi) · Social base (elite middle class vs broader middle class + students) · Religion in politics (separate vs symbols used by Tilak) · Contribution: Moderates = institution building, economic critique, constitutional precedents; Extremists = mass mobilization, boycott tactics, emotional awakening. Not binary — complementary phases. Bipan Chandra's view: "Both were necessary stages." Gandhi synthesized both.
Lal-Bal-Pal, 3P policy, constitutional agitation, swadeshi movement
UPSC Q4 · Contextual
How did the Partition of Bengal (1905) become a turning point in India's freedom struggle?
Answer Framework
Background: Curzon's real motive vs official justification. Bengali nationalist response. Swadeshi movement: boycott + national education + passive resistance. Communal dimension: Muslim League (1906) formed in its aftermath. Pan-Indian solidarity: anti-partition feeling spread beyond Bengal. Annulment (1911) = first major British concession to nationalist pressure. Long-term: Hindu-Muslim relations strained; seeds of 1947 partition. Legacy: Swadeshi methods influenced Gandhi's later programmes.
Oct 16 Day of Mourning, Tagore's Rakhi Bandhan, Bengal Natl College

Q7 Answer Framework — Tilak's Mass Nationalism
Before Tilak: INC = elite sessions, English-speaking audience. Tilak's innovations: Ganapati festival (1893) — community mobilization; Shivaji Mahotsav (1895) — historical pride; Kesari in Marathi — vernacular reach; Deccan Education Society (1884); "Swaraj" as emotional rallying cry. Result: Students, peasants, artisans entered nationalist consciousness. Limitations: religious symbolism alienated Muslims. Balance: mass awakening with communal complications. Gandhi later universalized this mass approach without religious exclusivism.
Lokamanya, Ganapati festival, Kesari sedition trials
