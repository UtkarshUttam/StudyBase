01

Foundation of Kinematics

Core definitions · Frame of reference · Scalars vs Vectors · Motion basics

🔬
What is Kinematics? DEFINITION
▶

📌 Definition

Branch of mechanics that describes the motion of objects without considering the cause (force) of motion. It deals with position, velocity, acceleration, and time.

No Force Position Time

🆚 Kinematics vs Dynamics

KinematicsDynamics
How objects moveWhy objects move
No forces involvedForces analyzed
Position, velocity, timeMass, force, energy
🌐 Frame of Reference

Definition

A coordinate system relative to which motion is measured. Motion is relative — same object can be in motion in one frame, at rest in another.

Inertial Frame

Frame at rest or moving with constant velocity. Newton's laws are valid. Example: ground frame (approximately).

Non-Inertial Frame

Accelerating or rotating frame. Newton's laws need pseudo forces. Example: accelerating car, rotating earth.

⚠️ CDS Trap: Frame of Reference

  • A person sitting in a moving train sees the platform moving — but the platform is at rest in ground frame.
  • CDS often asks: "Which observer sees the object at rest?" → The one moving with the object.

⚠️ Classic PYQ Trap

  • A car goes 60 km East, then 80 km North. Distance = 140 km. Displacement = 100 km (Pythagoras: √(60²+80²))
  • Average speed ≠ Average velocity (unless straight line, no reversal)
  • Instantaneous speed = magnitude of instantaneous velocity ✓ (Always true)
⚡
Acceleration — The Rate Changer VECTOR
▶
a = Δv / Δt = (v - u) / t
Rate of change of velocity
a = acceleration (m/s²)
v = final velocity (m/s)
u = initial velocity (m/s)
t = time (s)
a = dv/dt = d²x/dt²
Instantaneous acceleration (calculus form)

📊 Types of Acceleration

  • Uniform: Constant a → equations of motion apply
  • Non-uniform: Variable a → integration needed
  • Zero: Constant velocity (uniform motion)
  • Negative (Retardation): Deceleration — velocity decreasing
  • Centripetal: Toward center in circular motion

⚠️ Retardation Trap

Retardation = negative acceleration. A body can have negative acceleration but still move in positive direction (slowing down). CDS frequently tests this.

02

Types of Motion

Rectilinear · Circular · Projectile · Relative · Uniform · Non-uniform

📊
Classification of Motion OVERVIEW
▶

➡️ Rectilinear (Linear)

  • Motion along straight line
  • 1D — only one axis needed
  • Equations of motion fully apply
  • Example: free fall, car on highway
  • Graphs: straight or curved lines

🔄 Circular Motion

  • Path is a circle
  • Direction constantly changes
  • Speed may be constant, velocity changes
  • Centripetal acceleration always present
  • Example: fan blade, satellite orbit

🚀 Projectile Motion

  • 2D motion under gravity
  • Horizontal: uniform motion
  • Vertical: uniformly accelerated
  • Path: parabola
  • Example: ball thrown at angle, bomb dropped

↔️ Uniform Motion

  • Equal distances in equal time intervals
  • Constant velocity
  • Acceleration = 0
  • d-t graph: straight line
  • v-t graph: horizontal line

📈 Non-Uniform Motion

  • Unequal distances in equal time
  • Velocity changes (a ≠ 0)
  • d-t graph: curved line
  • Can be uniformly accelerated or random

🌊 Oscillatory Motion

  • Back-and-forth about mean position
  • Periodic in nature
  • Example: pendulum, spring-mass
  • Displacement changes sign

Equations of Motion

Three golden equations · Derivations · Sign convention · PYQ patterns

📐
The Three Golden Equations MUST KNOW
▶

Applicability Conditions: These equations apply ONLY when acceleration is uniform (constant) throughout the motion.

v = u + at
1st Equation — Velocity-Time
Derived from: a = (v-u)/t
Dimension: [LT⁻¹]
No 's' involved
Relation: v,u,a,t
s = ut + ½at²
2nd Equation — Displacement-Time
Derived from integrating v = u+at
Dimension: [L]
No final velocity
Relation: s,u,a,t
v² = u² + 2as
3rd Equation — Velocity-Displacement
Derived by eliminating t
Dimension: [L²T⁻²]
No time involved
Relation: v,u,a,s
📖 Quick Derivations

Graphical Derivation of v = u + at

On v-t graph, initial velocity = u (y-intercept). Slope = a.

v = u + (slope × t) = u + at ✓

Graphical Derivation of s = ut + ½at²

Area under v-t graph = displacement

Area = rectangle + triangle = ut + ½at² ✓

🎯 Special Applications

🍎 Free Fall (a = g = 10 m/s²)

v = u + gt
Taking downward as positive

• Object thrown up: u positive, a = −g (decelerating)
• Object dropped: u = 0, s = ½gt²
• Time to fall height h: t = √(2h/g)

🚗 Braking Distance

A vehicle at speed v brakes with deceleration a:

s = v² / 2a
From v² = u² + 2as with final v = 0

Braking distance ∝ v² (double speed → 4× distance)

⊕⊖ Sign Convention — Critical!

Sign Convention Rules

  • Choose positive direction first (up/right usually)
  • Stick to it throughout the problem
  • Displacement can be negative (opposite direction)
  • Acceleration negative when opposing motion

⚠️ Sign Convention Traps in CDS

  • Ball thrown up:
    u = +20 m/s, a = −10 m/s² → at top: v = 0,
    Time = 2s, max height = 20m
  • Don't make g positive when direction is already set as up-positive
  • s in v²=u²+2as is displacement not distance!
🏆 Bonus: Distance in nth Second
sₙ = u + a(n - ½)
Distance covered in the nth second
Derived: sₙ = s_total(t=n) − s_total(t=n-1)
Highly tested in NDA/CDS numericals
CDS PYQ PATTERN

Q: A body starts from rest. Find ratio of distances covered in 1st, 2nd, 3rd second.

Answer: s₁:s₂:s₃ = 1:3:5 (odd number series with u=0)

Using sₙ = 0 + a(n−½) → 1a/2 : 3a/2 : 5a/2 = 1:3:5

🧠 Memory: The 3 Equations — "VUS-SAV Triangle"

Graphs in Kinematics

d-t · s-t · v-t · a-t graphs · Slope · Area · Traps

📈
Graph Mastery — Slope & Area HIGH YIELD
▶
Slope of d-tSpeed (scalar)
Slope of s-tVelocity (vector)
Slope of v-tAcceleration
Area under v-tDisplacement
Area under a-tChange in velocity (Δv)
Area under s-tNo physical meaning!
DISTANCE-TIME GRAPH

Slope = speed. Steeper = faster. Horizontal = rest. Negative slope impossible (distance never decreases).

DISPLACEMENT-TIME GRAPH

Slope = velocity. Negative slope = returning. Horizontal = at rest. CDS trap: negative slope ≠ going back in time!

VELOCITY-TIME GRAPH

Slope = acceleration. Area under curve = displacement. Most tested graph in CDS!

ACCELERATION-TIME GRAPH

Area under a-t = change in velocity. Horizontal line = uniform acceleration. Most conceptually tested!

⚠️ Graph Traps in CDS/NDA

  • v-t graph: if line crosses time axis → body reverses direction
  • d-t graph can never have negative slope or go below x-axis (distance ≥ 0)
  • s-t graph negative slope ✓ (displacement can be negative)
  • Area under v-t can be negative (displacement) but not distance!
  • Curved v-t graph → non-uniform acceleration (not constant a)
  • Steeper slope on s-t doesn't mean larger acceleration, just higher velocity

Projectile Motion

Horizontal & Angular projection · Range · Height · Trajectory · Special angles

🚀
Projectile Motion — Complete Analysis MOST TESTED
▶

📋 Assumptions (State in CDS answers!)

  • ✓ Gravity is uniform (g = constant)
  • ✓ Air resistance neglected
  • ✓ Earth is flat (not rotating)
  • ✓ Horizontal velocity remains constant
  • ✓ Only gravity acts vertically
  • ✓ Initial acceleration = 0 horizontally

📦 Horizontal Projection (θ = 0°)

Object thrown horizontally with velocity u from height h

T = √(2h/g)
Time of flight
R = u·√(2h/g)
Horizontal range
v = √(u² + g²t²)
Velocity at time t

🎯 Angular Projection (at angle θ)

Object launched at angle θ with velocity u

T = 2u·sinθ / g
Time of flight
H = u²·sin²θ / 2g
Maximum height
R = u²·sin2θ / g
Horizontal range
y = x·tanθ − gx²/(2u²cos²θ)
Trajectory equation (parabola)

CDS EXAM TRAP

Complementary Angles: θ and (90°−θ) give the same horizontal range. So 30° and 60° give equal range. Very frequently asked in CDS and NDA!

Key: R(θ) = R(90°−θ) → Use R = u²sin2θ/g, sin2(90°−θ) = sin(180°−2θ) = sin2θ ✓

🔄 Symmetry in Projectile Motion

⏱️ Time Symmetry

Time to reach maximum height = T/2 = u·sinθ/g

Time of ascent = Time of descent

🎯 Velocity Symmetry

At same height on way up and way down: speed is same, horizontal velocity same, vertical velocity reversed in sign

📐 At Maximum Height

Vertical velocity = 0

Only horizontal component remains: v = u·cosθ

Acceleration = g (downward) always!

🧠 Projectile Formulas Memory: "T-H-R Rocket"

T = 2u sinθ / g  |  H = u² sin²θ / 2g  |  R = u² sin2θ / g

Twice the sin · Half with sin-squared · Range gets double angle

Relative Motion

Relative velocity · River-boat · Rain-man · Train problems

🚤
Relative Velocity & Applications VECTOR ADDITION
▶
v⃗_AB = v⃗_A − v⃗_B
Velocity of A relative to B
v⃗_BA = −v⃗_AB (opposite direction)

🚢 River-Boat Problem

Boat speed = v_b, River speed = v_r, Width = d

t = d / v_b
For minimum time (boat ⊥ river)
θ = sin⁻¹(v_r/v_b)
Angle to aim for straight crossing

Min time: boat perpendicular to river
Min drift: boat angled upstream at sin⁻¹(v_r/v_b)

🌧️ Rain-Man Problem

Rain velocity = v_r (vertical), Man velocity = v_m (horizontal)

v_rain_rel = √(v_r² + v_m²)
Relative velocity of rain w.r.t. man

Man should hold umbrella at angle:
tan θ = v_m/v_r (forward tilt)
To keep dry, tilt toward direction of motion

🚂 Train Problems

Trains A and B:

  • Same direction: v_rel = |v_A − v_B|
  • Opposite direction: v_rel = v_A + v_B
  • Time to cross = (L_A + L_B) / v_rel
  • Platform crossing: t = (L_train + L_platform) / v_train

⚠️ Relative Velocity Traps

  • Two trains moving same direction at same speed: relative velocity = 0 (they appear stationary to each other)
  • Velocity of A w.r.t. B = −(velocity of B w.r.t. A)
  • Rain problem: if man stands still, he sees rain falling at angle θ to vertical = tan⁻¹(v_m/v_r)
  • For straight crossing: boat needs to aim upstream, NOT perpendicular

Common CDS/UPSC Traps

Sign errors · Scalar-vector confusion · Graph misreads · Exam pitfalls

🚨
Top Exam Traps — Don't Lose These Marks! CRITICAL
▶

Trap 1: Scalar vs Vector

  • Speed is scalar → CANNOT be negative
  • Velocity is vector → CAN be negative
  • Distance is scalar → CANNOT be negative
  • "Speed increased by 5 m/s" ≠ "velocity increased by 5 m/s"

Trap 2: Average Speed vs Velocity

  • Average speed = Total distance / Total time
  • Average velocity = Total displacement / Total time
  • If return journey: displacement = 0 → avg velocity = 0!
  • Avg speed for equal distances: 2v₁v₂/(v₁+v₂)

Trap 3: Free Fall Confusion

  • g = 9.8 m/s² (use 10 for CDS unless stated)
  • At maximum height: velocity = 0, but a = g ≠ 0!
  • Heavier body does NOT fall faster (ignore air resistance)
  • Object thrown up: acceleration is ALWAYS downward

Trap 4: Projectile Misconceptions

  • Horizontal velocity is CONSTANT throughout
  • Acceleration at any point = g (downward), including at peak
  • Path is parabolic, NOT circular
  • R(30°) = R(60°) — same range, different paths!

Trap 5: Graph Reading Errors

  • Steeper slope on s-t doesn't = more acceleration
  • Area under v-t = displacement (not distance if crosses axis)
  • Area under s-t = no physical meaning
  • Slope of s-t = velocity, NOT speed (can be negative)

Trap 6: Circular Motion

  • Uniform circular motion ≠ constant velocity
  • Centripetal force is resultant, not an extra force
  • Centrifugal force is pseudo-force (non-inertial frame only)
  • Speed constant → KE constant, but momentum changes!

Practice & Application

Mini solved examples · CDS-style numericals · Speed-solving methods

🧮
Solved Examples — CDS Style PRACTICE
▶
EXAMPLE 1

A ball is dropped from a height of 80m. Find the velocity just before hitting the ground. (g = 10 m/s²)

v² = 2gh = 2×10×80 = 1600 → v = 40 m/s

Use v²=u²+2as, u=0, s=80m, a=g=10

EXAMPLE 2

A projectile is fired at 30 m/s at 30°. Find range. (g=10 m/s²)

R = u²sin2θ/g = (30)²×sin60°/10 = 900×0.866/10 = 77.9 m

EXAMPLE 3

A car decelerates from 20 m/s to rest in 4 seconds. Find braking distance.

a = (0−20)/4 = −5 m/s². s = v²/2|a| = 400/10 = 40 m

EXAMPLE 4

Two trains 100m and 150m long travel at 36 km/h and 54 km/h in opposite directions. Find crossing time.

v_rel = 36+54 = 90 km/h = 25 m/s. t = (100+150)/25 = 10 s

20 Probable CDS Prelims Questions

Click each question to reveal the answer and explanation

Q1
A body is thrown vertically upward with velocity u. The ratio of distances covered by it in the 1st and 3rd second is:

Answer: (2u−g) : (2u−5g)

Using sₙ = u + a(n−½), with a = −g:
s₁ = u − g/2 = (2u−g)/2 · s₃ = u − 5g/2 = (2u−5g)/2
Ratio = (2u−g):(2u−5g)

Q2
A projectile is fired with velocity u at angle θ. For maximum range, θ should be:

Answer: 45°

R = u²sin2θ/g. This is maximum when sin2θ = 1 → 2θ = 90° → θ = 45°. R_max = u²/g.

Q3
A ball is dropped from height H and another is thrown horizontally from same height. Which reaches ground first?

Answer: Both reach simultaneously

Vertical motion is independent of horizontal motion. Both have same initial vertical velocity (zero) and same vertical acceleration (g). Time = √(2H/g) for both.

Q4
A body starts from rest and moves with uniform acceleration. The ratio of distances covered in 1st, 2nd, and 3rd second is:

Answer: 1 : 3 : 5

Using sₙ = u + a(n−½) with u=0: s₁=a/2, s₂=3a/2, s₃=5a/2. Ratio = 1:3:5. This is the classic odd-number series!

Q5
Two projectiles are fired at angles 30° and 60° with same speed. Their ranges are:

Answer: Equal

Complementary angles (30°+60°=90°) give equal ranges. R = u²sin2θ/g. sin(2×30°)=sin60°=sin(2×60°)=sin120°. Both equal √3/2.

Q6
A car moving at 72 km/h applies brakes and stops in 8 seconds. The retardation is:

Answer: 2.5 m/s²

72 km/h = 20 m/s. v = u + at → 0 = 20 − a×8 → a = 20/8 = 2.5 m/s²

Q7
The slope of a displacement-time graph gives:

Answer: Velocity

Slope = Δs/Δt = velocity. It can be positive, negative, or zero. Note: slope of distance-time gives speed (always ≥ 0).

Q8
A stone is thrown vertically upward with 20 m/s. How high will it go? (g = 10 m/s²)

Answer: 20 m

v² = u² − 2gh → 0 = 400 − 2(10)h → h = 400/20 = 20 m

Q9
In uniform circular motion, what remains constant?

Answer: Speed (not velocity)

Direction changes continuously, so velocity (vector) changes. Speed (magnitude) remains constant. Angular velocity, time period, and centripetal acceleration magnitude are also constant.

Q10
A man walks 3 km North then 4 km East. His displacement is:

Answer: 5 km (Northeast)

Displacement = √(3² + 4²) = √(9+16) = √25 = 5 km. The Pythagorean 3-4-5 triangle! Distance = 7 km, Displacement = 5 km.

Q11
Area under a velocity-time graph represents:

Answer: Displacement

∫v dt = displacement (not distance). If the graph goes below the time axis, that area is negative (backward displacement). For distance, take |area|.

Q12
A body has zero displacement but non-zero distance. What type of motion is this?

Answer: Motion with return to starting point

Example: going 5 km and returning. Distance = 10 km, Displacement = 0. Circular path also gives zero displacement for one complete loop.

Q13
Which equation of motion does NOT contain the final velocity?

Answer: s = ut + ½at²

The 2nd equation s = ut + ½at² contains s, u, a, t — no final velocity v. The 1st has v, the 3rd has v².

Q14
A stone thrown at 45° has range R. If thrown with double the speed, range becomes:

Answer: 4R

R = u²sin2θ/g ∝ u². Doubling u → R' = (2u)²sin2θ/g = 4u²sin2θ/g = 4R.

Q15
Two trains move in opposite directions at 40 km/h and 60 km/h. What is relative velocity?

Answer: 100 km/h

Opposite directions: v_rel = v_A + v_B = 40 + 60 = 100 km/h. Same direction would give |40−60| = 20 km/h.

Q16
At maximum height in projectile motion, the acceleration is:

Answer: g (downward)

Acceleration due to gravity acts throughout the motion — even at maximum height where vertical velocity is zero. a = g = 9.8 m/s² downward always.

Q17
A body covers equal distances in equal time intervals. This is:

Answer: Uniform motion

Equal distance in equal time = constant velocity = uniform motion. Acceleration = zero. d-t graph is a straight line with constant slope.

Q18
A river 100m wide flows at 5 m/s. A boat can do 10 m/s in still water. Minimum time to cross:

Answer: 10 seconds

For minimum time, boat aims perpendicular to river. t = width/v_boat = 100/10 = 10 s. (River speed doesn't affect crossing time in perpendicular case.)

Q19
The trajectory of a projectile is a:

Answer: Parabola

Equation: y = x·tanθ − gx²/(2u²cos²θ). This is of the form y = ax − bx², which is a parabola (second-degree equation in x).

Q20
A body starts from rest with acceleration 2 m/s². Distance covered in 5th second is:

Answer: 9 m

sₙ = u + a(n − ½) = 0 + 2(5 − 0.5) = 2 × 4.5 = 9 m

10 UPSC-Level Conceptual Questions

Analytical depth · Assertion-Reason · Conceptual clarity

U1
Assertion (A): A particle can have zero velocity but non-zero acceleration.
Reason (R): Acceleration depends on change in velocity, not velocity itself.

Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

At the highest point of vertical throw: v = 0 but a = g ≠ 0. Also, in SHM at mean position: acceleration is proportional to displacement (non-zero) but velocity is maximum. The reason perfectly explains the assertion.

U2
Why does a bomb dropped from a horizontally flying aircraft travel ahead of the aircraft rather than falling straight down?

Because the bomb retains the horizontal velocity of the aircraft.

At the moment of release, the bomb has the same horizontal velocity as the aircraft. Since no horizontal force acts (ignoring air resistance), this horizontal velocity is maintained. The bomb follows a parabolic path and remains ahead (horizontally below) the aircraft throughout the fall.

U3
Can the magnitude of average velocity of a body be greater than its average speed? Justify.

No — average speed ≥ |average velocity| always.

Average speed = total distance/time. Average velocity = displacement/time. Since distance ≥ |displacement| (by triangle inequality — shortest path is straight line), average speed ≥ |average velocity|. Equality holds only for straight-line motion without reversal.

U4
Explain why the equations of motion (v = u + at, etc.) are not applicable when acceleration is non-uniform.

These are derived assuming constant acceleration throughout.

The derivation uses: Δv = a·Δt (only valid for constant a) and area under v-t = ut + ½at² (only for straight v-t line). If a varies with time, the v-t graph is curved, and we need integration: s = ∫v dt, where v = ∫a dt. The simple formulas break down entirely.

U5
A satellite in circular orbit has constant speed. Yet it undergoes acceleration. Explain this apparent contradiction.

Acceleration doesn't require change in speed — change in direction is sufficient.

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity (vector). In circular orbit, the direction of velocity changes constantly toward the center. This centripetal acceleration = v²/r is provided by gravity. Speed remains constant, but the vector velocity changes — so acceleration is real and non-zero.

U6
Why does a projectile launched at 45° achieve maximum range? What happens at angles greater than 45°?

Because sin2θ is maximized at θ=45°, giving sin90°=1.

R = u²sin2θ/g. Since −1 ≤ sin2θ ≤ 1, maximum range when sin2θ=1, i.e., 2θ=90°, θ=45°. Above 45°: more height, less range (vertical component "wastes" time). Below 45°: more horizontal, but less time aloft. At 45°: optimal balance. Note: R(θ) = R(90°−θ) shows the symmetry.

U7
Assertion: In the absence of air resistance, a heavier body and lighter body dropped from the same height hit the ground simultaneously. Reason: Acceleration due to gravity is independent of mass.

Both A and R are correct, R explains A.

From Newton's 2nd Law: F = ma, and F = mg (gravity). So a = g — the mass cancels! This is the equivalence principle demonstrated by Galileo. All bodies fall with the same acceleration g regardless of mass (in vacuum). Air resistance breaks this because drag force is not proportional to mass in the same way.

U8
How does the concept of relative motion explain why rain appears to fall diagonally when viewed from a moving vehicle?

Relative velocity of rain with respect to the moving observer has both vertical and horizontal components.

In ground frame: rain falls vertically at v_r. Person in vehicle moves horizontally at v_m. Relative velocity of rain w.r.t. person = v_rain − v_person. The horizontal component is −v_m (backward direction for person). So the person sees rain at angle θ = tan⁻¹(v_m/v_r) from vertical, appearing to fall forward/diagonally.

U9
Is it possible for a body to have: (a) constant speed but changing velocity? (b) constant velocity but changing speed? Justify.

(a) Yes — circular motion. (b) No — impossible.

(a) In uniform circular motion, |v| = speed is constant, but direction changes → velocity changes. (b) Velocity is a vector whose magnitude is speed. If velocity is constant (fixed magnitude AND direction), speed (=magnitude of velocity) cannot change. If speed changed, the magnitude of velocity would change, meaning velocity changed — contradiction.

U10
Explain the significance of the parabolic trajectory in projectile motion. How is this used in military ballistics?

The trajectory y = x·tanθ − gx²/(2u²cos²θ) is a parabola; range and height can be precisely calculated.

The parabolic path results from the superposition of uniform horizontal motion and uniformly accelerated vertical motion. In ballistics: guns are aimed at 45° for maximum range; for specific targets at known distances, angles can be calculated using R = u²sin2θ/g. Two solutions exist for any sub-maximum range (θ and 90°−θ). Artillery elevation tables are essentially pre-computed range-angle tables based on this physics.
